# The before-and-after report

The single artifact that makes this uncomparable to "my nephew builds websites
for $400." A nephew cannot produce one. Neither can a phone room, because
producing it requires having looked at the business before the call.

Two documents, same format, same measurements. **Before** is built during
prospecting and shown on the call. **After** goes out the day the site is live
and becomes month zero of the monthly report.

The discipline: **every line is a fact with a screenshot or a timestamp behind
it.** No adjectives, no scores out of ten, no grades. The moment it contains an
opinion it becomes a sales document and stops working.

---

## Section 1 — Can they be found

| Measurement | How |
|---|---|
| Google, "<trade> <town>" | Position of their own site, or absent. Screenshot. |
| Google, their business name | Does their own site outrank the directories? Screenshot. |
| Map pack, "<trade> near me" | Present or absent. Screenshot. |
| ChatGPT, "who should I hire for <trade> in <town>" | Named or not. Full answer, screenshot. |
| Gemini, same question | Named or not. Screenshot. |
| Claude, same question | Named or not. Screenshot. |
| ChatGPT, "tell me about <business name>" | What it knows, or "I don't have information about that business." |

That last line is the one that sells this. An owner who hears their own business
name returned as unknown does not need the problem explained.

## Section 2 — What Google knows

| Field | Recorded as |
|---|---|
| Profile claimed | Yes / no / claimed by someone else |
| Categories | Listed, with wrong or missing ones named |
| Website link | Present, absent, or pointing somewhere wrong |
| Hours | Present, absent, or contradicting the site |
| Description | Character count against the 750 available |
| Services listed | Count |
| Photos | Count, and date of most recent |
| Reviews | Count, average, date of most recent, owner responses yes/no |
| Q&A | Answered, unanswered, or empty |

## Section 3 — What the website communicates

| Check | Recorded as |
|---|---|
| Site exists | Yes / no / parked / broken |
| Loads on mobile | Seconds to first paint, on a phone, not a laptop |
| Trade named in the title | Yes / no |
| Towns served, on a page | Yes / no |
| Services, each with a page | Count |
| Real job photos | Count, or stock |
| Structured data present | Yes / no. This is the one nobody else measures. |

## Section 4 — Can they be contacted

**The section that carries the second half of the promise, and the one that
costs nothing but HTML to fix.**

| Check | Recorded as |
|---|---|
| Phone number visible without scrolling | Yes / no |
| Phone number is tap-to-call on mobile | Yes / no |
| Contact form exists | Yes / no |
| Form submits successfully | **Tested. Send one.** |
| Where the form goes | Email address, and whether anyone reads it |
| Text option offered | Yes / no |
| Google listing call button | Configured / not |
| Quote request path | Count the taps from landing to sent |

Most leaks live here, and none of them need software to fix. A phone number that
is not a `tel:` link on a phone is a lost job, every time, for free.

## The after

Identical document, re-measured, same day the site goes live. Three columns:
**before, after, what changed.**

Rules:

- **Do not re-measure rankings on day one.** They will not have moved, saying so
  builds the credibility that makes the month-three number believable, and
  claiming otherwise is the thing that makes people distrust this whole category.
- Section 4 should be entirely fixed on day one. That is the honest immediate win.
- Section 1 is the baseline everything future is compared against.
- Send it whether or not it is flattering.

## What it is for, beyond the sale

It is the measurement layer. Section 4 tested at month three tells you where a
business is still losing calls it already earned — which is the only honest
reason to ever sell them anything else. No leak, no second product.
