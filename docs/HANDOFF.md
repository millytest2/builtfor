## 3. The offer (final)

| Rung | Offer | Buyer |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Free report card** — $0 | Not a product. The door. One command. |
| 1 | **Starter Site** — $500 + $99/mo | No website at all. One call or walk-in. 4 days. |
| 2 | **Booking Build** — $1,500–2,500 + $349/mo | One job worth $2,000+. 14 days, two phases. |
| 3 | **Growth Systems** — +$200–400/mo each | Existing clients, sold on evidence only. |

**Non-negotiable rules:**
- **Never quote a site price alone.** Two numbers in one breath: "five hundred to
  get set up, then ninety-nine a month."
- **We host it** — that makes the monthly the utility bill, not a favor.
- **Tracking number on every site** (~$1.50/mo). Proves the site works AND surfaces
  the data that sells Rung 3.
- **Monthly differs by rung.** A $500 client won't carry $349/mo. Don't try.
- **14 days, not 7.** Every dependency (photos, testimonials, logins, approvals) is
  on the client's side. Photos alone routinely take a week. Day 1 starts when
  materials arrive, not when they pay.

**What you are NOT selling:** AI, SEO, GEO, AEO, funnels, automations, growth
strategy, digital transformation. Internally use all of it; externally sell a
visible fix. Say "AI-powered" and you become the fifteenth caller that week.

**Rung 3 order (never invent a custom AI project):** missed-inquiry recovery →
estimate/quote follow-up → dormant-customer reactivation. One at a time, only when
their own data names it.

### The monthly: bundled, but nearly zero-touch (resolves a real contradiction)

Two positions were in play and they cannot both be true:
- *"Monthly on every site, never quote a price alone"* — correct on the **mechanic**.
  An optional monthly is a monthly nobody buys. Client #1 proves it: quoted as
  "a website for $400," and the recurring conversation was dead on arrival.
- *"Don't force fake recurring, this becomes a maintenance agency"* — correct on the
  **substance**. Google posting, review responses, directory cleanup and monthly SEO
  tweaks across 30 clients is Social Hog with worse margins.

**The resolution: bundle it, and make the monthly buy infrastructure, not labor.**

| The $99/mo buys | The $99/mo does NOT buy |
|---|---|
| Hosting, SSL, domain pointing | Google Business Profile posting |
| Uptime + security + backups | Review responses |
| Tracking number + call log | Directory cleanup |
| Form delivery stays working | Monthly SEO "tweaks" |
| One small edit per month (hours, photo, service) | Content, social, reporting decks |

Labor budget: **under 20 minutes per client per month.** If a care plan ever needs
more than that, the plan is mispriced or the promise is wrong — fix the promise, not
the hours.

**Why $99 and not $149:** the monthly's job right now is to *survive the close*. Under
$100 it reads as a utility bill and doesn't trigger a negotiation. **Raise to $149
only when you can point at a tracking-number call log and say "this site produced 11
calls last month."** Evidence buys the price increase; hope doesn't.

Real recurring money later comes from Rung 3 systems that keep working for the
customer, not from doing more chores for $99.

## 4. Targeting — niche by CONDITION, not industry

Nothing about a website + Google profile + maintenance is industry-specific.
Declaring a vertical now shrinks the pool while claiming unearned expertise.

**The condition (all six, checkable from Google Maps in ~1 minute):**
1. Owner-operated, ~1–15 employees
2. One job worth **$1,000+** (this is what rules out barbers and cafés)
3. **10–49 good reviews** ← see the correction below
4. Phone- / estimate-dependent
5. Reachable owner
6. No site, weak site, or no contact path — a gap you can point at before speaking

**Three phases:**
- **Phase 1 (now):** condition niche. "We work with good local service businesses
  that are better at their work than at the internet."
- **Phase 2 (~10 clients):** problem specialization. Missed inquiries / unclosed
  estimates / dormant customers.
- **Phase 3 (only if earned):** vertical specialization, around the *repeated
  expensive problem you discovered* — never an industry that sounded good upfront.

### The review-count correction (important — this reversed twice)
Original scoring treated **few reviews** as the "invisible" signal. That was then
flipped to favor **high reviews** as "proven." Both were wrong at the extremes:

| Band | Count in data | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 2–9 | 43 | Unproven, may be one guy with a truck |
| **10–24** | **32** | **Best — established, still hungry** |
| **25–49** | **16** | **Good — solid, still has capacity** |
| 50–99 | 9 | Busy |
| 100+ | 6 | **Worst — at capacity, no pain, already declined websites for years** |

A business with 152 five-star reviews and no website has had money and been pitched
a dozen times. They didn't do it. **That's a revealed preference, not a gap.**

**The best lead of all is one the tool can't currently find: a business that HAS a
website and it's bad.** Want is already proven — they spent money and got let down.
That's intent plus grievance. Add this filter when the API is restored.

### The 10-second disqualifier — ask this early, every call
> **"Have you ever had a website, or just never got around to it?"**

- *"Had one, it was useless"* → **best lead you'll get**
- *"Been meaning to"* → **live lead**, inertia not refusal
- *"Don't need one, I'm booked solid"* → **hang up politely.** Don't burn 20 minutes.

  Sell them *later* with conversion case studies.
- **Healthcare, legal, regulated** — specifically for reactivation. Consent and
  privacy complexity you don't need while validating.

## 9. The call script — goal is a booked 10 minutes, not a sale

**1. Open (honesty disarms)**
> "Hey, is this [Name]? I'll be straight with you, this is kind of a cold call. You
> can hang up if you want. Gimme twenty seconds and then tell me to get lost?"

**2. Disqualify immediately**
> "Quick one first — have you ever had a website, or just never got around to it?"

**3. The observation, then STOP TALKING**
> "So I looked you up before I called. You've got [34] five-star reviews — people
> clearly like you. And when I went to click through and learn more, there was
> nowhere to go. You're doing great on Google Maps and you're basically invisible
> everywhere else. Did you know it looked like that?"

**4. One discovery question**
- Tree: *"When everybody's out on a job, what happens to the phone?"*
- Gates: *"How many estimates do you send a month — and who follows up on the ones that don't come back?"*
- Septic: *"How many tanks have you pumped over the years?"*

Write the answer down. That's Rung 3.

**5. The booking close — this is the whole call**
> "Here's all I'd suggest. Let me put together a quick example of what your site
> could look like. Takes me a day, costs you nothing. Then I'll call you and walk
> you through it in ten minutes — hate it, you've lost nothing.
> **You around Thursday morning, or is Friday afternoon better?**"

Why it works: the appointment is to see **their own thing**, it's **ten minutes**,
and you offer **two specific times** instead of "when works for you."

**6. Lock it**
> "Perfect — Thursday at 9. Best number this one? And real quick so the example
> isn't generic: what kind of work do you actually *want* more of?"

Text immediately: *"Miles — good talking to you. Thursday 9am, I'll have that
example ready. 310-606-9788."*

### Gates = walk in, don't call
> "Are you the owner? I'm Miles, I'm local, over in [area]. Pulled up your Google
> before I came by — twenty-one five-star reviews and no website. I already built
> you an example. Mind if I show you on my phone real quick?"

### Objections — every one pivots back to the booking
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "How much?" | "Less than you'd think — a few hundred to get going. Let me show you the example first, then the number makes sense or it doesn't. **Thursday or Friday?**" |
| "Word of mouth is all I need" | "Best customers there are, honestly. But even referrals Google you first to check you're real, and right now there's nothing to find. **Ten minutes Thursday?**" |
| "I'm booked solid" | "Good problem — then it's about *better* jobs, not more. Being easy to find lets you be pickier. **Worth ten minutes?**" |
| "I'm too busy right now" | "Totally — that's why I'm asking about Thursday, not now. **Morning or afternoon?**" |
| "Send me info" | "I'll do better — I'll build the actual thing. What's your email? And **Thursday or Friday** to walk through it?" |
| "My nephew's building one" | "Ha, that's how half of them start — usually stalls at eighty percent because he's busy. No shade. Mine's done this week." |

**Voicemail, then text within 5 minutes.** Most won't answer.
**Call times:** trades answer **6:30–8am** and **4–6pm** local. Midday they're on a
job. Start Pacific, work east.

## 11. The honest critique — carry this forward

**Nine modules, 23 vertical configs, 660 leads pulled, five PDFs, and $400 of
revenue — which came from a phone call to someone Daniel already knew.**

The constraint is **sales, not tooling.** Every turn produced another tool because
building feels like progress and it's the fastest thing to produce. It was a shared
mistake: asked for and delivered.

Also worth remembering: **the target changed about six times** (HVAC/plumbing → all
local business → barbershops/cafés → gap-fixing → off-radar trades → premium
people-businesses → career sector → condition-niche). The config-driven build
survived every pivot; the positioning didn't. You can't compound niche depth while
changing niche weekly — which is exactly why the answer landed on condition-niching
plus a scoreboard.

**Stop building. The next real milestone is not a feature or a better plan — it's
the first stranger who isn't a friend of Daniel's handing over money.** Everything
needed to get that is already in this repo.
