## 3. The offer (final)

One sentence, said out loud, to a stranger:

> **"It's four hundred to get you set up — that's the site, your Google profile fixed
> up, a tracking number so you can hear every call it brings in, click-to-call so it
> rings your cell. Then three forty-nine a month and we keep the whole thing running.
> First month's included, so nothing else comes out until day thirty. You can cancel
> any time and the site's yours either way."**

If they want to buy it outright: **"Yeah, six hundred, you own it, you're on your own
after. Most guys go the other way since it's cheaper to start and we keep it working."**

### The ladder — using the playbook's inverted discount, which is better

`docs/playbook.md` §2b already solved this and it beats a plain anchor-and-downsell.
**Make the monthly the cheaper path, not the add-on:**

| Option | Price | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Website only | **$600 one-time** | Site, they own it, no support. **Deliberately the worse deal.** |
| **Growth Plan** (default) | **$400 setup + $349/mo** | Site + hosting + Google profile + tracking number + missed-call text-back + reviews + monthly call log. **First month included.** |

Four things make the monthly stick, and only one of them is persuasion:
1. **Bundled from the first sentence.** There is nothing to upsell later because it was
   never separate.
2. **We host it.** If they own the hosting the monthly is a favor they can cancel. If we
   host, it's the utility bill. Structural.
3. **The monthly is what makes the site work.** "The site is the storefront; the monthly
   is what brings people through the door."
4. **Inverted discount.** The plan costs *less* upfront than buying the site outright,
   so choosing the monthly is the cheap choice, not the expensive one.

| | Offer | Price | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Door | Ring Test + free mockup + audit report card | $0 | Everyone |
| **Default** | **Growth Plan** | **$400 + $349/mo** | Every sale |
| Alternative | Website only | $600 one-time | Only if they insist |
| Fix | Visibility Fix (GBP, citations, reviews) | $397 | Presence is broken but a site exists |
| Add-ons | missed-call → estimate follow-up → reactivation → review engine | +$200–400/mo each | One at a time, on their own data |

**Caveat: $349/mo has never been said to anyone.** Neither has $249. The price is a
hypothesis either way, so use the structure the repo already reasoned through and let
the call log settle the number.

### Why this is hard to compete with

| Competitor | What they do | Why they can't match this |
|---|---|---|
| Local agency | $3–5K build, $500/mo, 6-week timeline | Cost base. They have staff; BFMS's marginal cost per client is ~$3/mo. |
| Wix / DIY | $0 + 40 hours the owner doesn't have | They're selling software; BFMS sells it done. |
| **Angi / Thumbtack / Yelp Ads** | **$50–100 per shared lead, forever** | **This is the real competitor. See below.** |
| Nephew with a laptop | Free, stalls at 80% | "Mine's done in 14 days." |
| Anyone | — | **Pay-on-launch. Cancel anytime. You own the domain.** Incumbents can't match that without breaking their own retention model, which depends on hostage-holding. |

**The lock-in nobody else offers is the reverse of lock-in.** They own the domain and
can walk. Say it early, out loud. It disarms the "I got burned by a marketing guy"
reflex that is standing between you and most of these sales.

### The competitor that matters: rented leads

Research finding, verified across every LA prospect checked: these businesses are
already on Thumbtack, Angi, Houzz, Networx, BuildZoom, Nextdoor and BBB. They are
**already paying for lead acquisition**, per lead, shared with three competitors, at
$50–100 a pop for trades work. It is the most hated line item in the trades.

So the frame is not "you need a website." It's:

> "You're renting your leads. Every one of those costs you sixty bucks and gets sent
> to two other guys at the same time. I'm selling you the version you own."

At $249/mo, the Lead Line costs less than **four** Thumbtack leads. One recovered
tree job is $800–2,500. The math closes itself, and it's a math they already do.

### The monthly: bundled, but nearly zero-touch

| The monthly buys | It does NOT buy |
|---|---|
| Hosting, SSL, domain | Google Business Profile posting |
| Uptime, security, backups | Review responses |
| Tracking number + monthly call log | Directory cleanup |
| Missed-call text-back ($249 tier) | Monthly SEO "tweaks" |
| One small edit per month | Content, social, reporting decks |

**Labor budget: under 20 minutes per client per month, batched to one day.** If a care
plan needs more, the promise is wrong — fix the promise, not the hours. This is the
firewall against becoming Social Hog. Making the monthly *optional* is not the fix;
an optional monthly is one nobody buys.

**Never quote a setup price alone. Two numbers, one breath.**
**14 days, not 7.** Every dependency is on the client's side. Day 1 starts when
materials arrive, not when they pay.

**What you are NOT selling:** AI, SEO, GEO, AEO, funnels, automations, growth
strategy. Internally use all of it. Say "AI-powered" and you become the fifteenth
caller that week.

site. A website alone is not disqualifying; a *good* website is. A real custom domain
with real pages and real reviews means someone is already being paid to help them, and
you are competing rather than filling a gap.

**The 10-second disqualifier, ask it every call:**
> **"Have you ever had a website, or just never got around to it?"**

- *"Had one, it was useless"* → **best lead you'll get** (this is what Ryan said)
- *"Been meaning to"* → live lead, inertia not refusal
- *"Don't need one, I'm booked solid"* → hang up politely

> "Searching septic / fence / monument in small towns returned **only businesses that

## 6. The unique mechanism: The Ring Test

Before pitching anyone, **call their business line twice** at two different times and
write down what happens. Roughly a third of calls to small trades go unanswered.

Then the open writes itself:

> "I called you Tuesday at 2 and again Thursday morning. Both went to voicemail. I'm
> not calling to give you a hard time — that's normal, you were on a job. I'm calling
> because I can tell you what those two calls were probably worth."

Nobody else does this. It is free, it takes ninety seconds, it is evidence about
*their* business gathered before the first sentence, and it makes the missed-call
text-back a solution to a problem you already proved rather than a feature you're
describing. **This is the mechanism. Lead with it.**

If they *do* answer both times, that's fine: "You picked up both times, which already
puts you ahead of most. So the question's different for you —" and go to the site.

**One platform. Three templates. One intake. One revision. Never negotiate the process.**

| 3–8 | Build from template | Miles (clients 1–5), **contractor (client 6+)** | 2–4 hrs |
| 9 | Preview link sent. **One revision round, one call, time-boxed.** | Miles | 30 min |
| 12–14 | Launch: domain, tracking number live, forms tested, call log on | Miles | 45 min |
| 14 | **Handoff call** — before/after, hand over access, ask for testimonial + 2 referrals *in the same call* | Miles | 20 min |

**Stack (pick once, never revisit):** static site from the existing config-driven
generator → Cloudflare Pages or Netlify (hosting ≈ $0) → domain ~$12/yr → tracking
number ~$1.50/mo + usage → missed-call text-back on Twilio ~$15/mo.
**Marginal cost per client: roughly $3/mo.** Gross margin on $99 is ~97%, on $249
~94%. That cost base is the reason nobody can undercut this.

**Care day: the first Monday of the month.** Every client's edit, every check, batched
into one block. 40 clients × 20 min = 13 hours, one day, once. Not 40 interruptions.

**Never:** custom scope, second revision round, a feature invented for one client, a
platform change, or a promise made on a call that isn't on this table.

## 9. The call script

**1. Open — the Ring Test**
> "Hey, is this [Name]? I'll be straight, this is a cold call, you can hang up. Before
> I do anything else — I called you Tuesday afternoon and again this morning, and both
> times it went to voicemail. Not giving you a hard time, you were working. Can I tell
> you why I noticed?"

**2. Disqualify**
> "Quick one — have you ever had a website, or just never got around to it?"

**3. The observation, then STOP TALKING**
> "You've got [24] five-star reviews, people clearly like you. But your site's on a
> free Wix address and it hasn't been touched since [year]. So you're winning on
> reviews and losing everywhere else. Did you know that's what it looks like?"

**4. One discovery question** — write the answer down verbatim, it's the Revenue
Recovery sale later
- Tree: *"When everybody's out on a job, what happens to the phone?"*
- Iron/gates: *"How many estimates go out a month, and who chases the ones that don't come back?"*
- Septic: *"How many tanks have you pumped over the years?"*
- Anyone on Thumbtack/Angi: *"What are you paying per lead on Thumbtack right now?"*

**5. The booking close — this is the whole call**
> "Here's all I'd suggest. Let me build a real example of your site. Takes me a day,
> costs you nothing, and if you hate it you've lost nothing. Then I walk you through
> it in ten minutes. **You around Thursday morning, or is Friday afternoon better?**"

**6. Lock it**
> "Perfect, Thursday at 9. Best number this one? And so the example isn't generic —
> what kind of work do you actually *want* more of?"

Text within 5 minutes: *"Miles — good talking to you. Thursday 9am, I'll have that
example ready. 310-606-9788."*

## 11. Designing it to be sellable (do this from client #1)

A buyer of a small local-services business pays for **MRR, documented systems, and low
owner-dependence** — roughly 2.5–4× annual SDE, or for MRR-heavy micro-businesses
~24–40× monthly net profit. Project revenue is worth close to nothing on exit;
recurring revenue is worth nearly all of it.

**Which means the $99/mo skipped on client #1 was not $1,188 of forgone cash. At exit
multiples it was about $3,000 of enterprise value destroyed in one sentence.** That is
the real argument for never dropping the monthly again.

Set these up now, while they're free:

- [ ] Separate business bank account and bookkeeping from dollar one. Nothing kills a
      small sale price faster than commingled books.
- [ ] Every client on **auto-billed Stripe subscription, card on file**, month-to-month
      written agreement. "MRR on a card" is the literal thing being bought.
- [ ] Track **churn** monthly. A buyer's first question is retention, and `kept_monthly_60d`
      in `vertical_scoreboard.csv` already exists for it.
- [ ] **No client over 10% of revenue.**
- [ ] Nothing hosted on personal accounts. Company-owned registrar, Cloudflare, Stripe,
      Twilio, Google Workspace.
- [ ] SOPs written as you go. The over-built repo finally pays off here: documented,
      transferable delivery is a real multiple-raiser.
- [ ] Clients' relationship is with **BFMS**, not with Miles personally. Use the
      hello@ address, not the cell, on everything after the sale.

**Exit shape:** at $40K/mo revenue and ~$30K/mo net, SDE is ~$360K → a plausible
**$900K–1.4M**. At the $10K level, net ~$7K/mo → SDE ~$84K → ~$210–330K. Either
outcome funds UPath without a raise. Even if nothing sells, the P&L is the credential.

## 14. The critique to carry forward

lifetime dials**, all to barbershops, in two afternoons two months apart, producing
one warm lead that was never called back.

changed about seven times.** The config-driven build survived every pivot; the
positioning didn't. You cannot compound niche depth while changing niche weekly, which
is why this lands on a condition filter, a scoreboard, and a search operator instead of
a declared vertical.

**The next real milestone is not a feature, a price change, or a better plan. It is the
first stranger who isn't a friend of Daniel's handing over money.** Everything needed
for that is in this repo and has been for weeks.
