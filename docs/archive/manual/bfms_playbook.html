1. The condition niche

The correction that matters most: we are niching by business condition, not by industry — because the offer isn't industry-specific yet.

Why not "the tree-service growth company"

Nothing about a website, a Google profile and a maintenance plan is tree-specific. Declaring a vertical now would shrink the prospect pool while claiming expertise we haven't earned. The honest read: our competitive advantage isn't industry knowledge — it's the ability to find an owner with an obvious problem, make it understandable, sell a simple fix, and then discover a more valuable problem through the relationship. That's the Social Hog muscle, except now we own the customer.

The condition — this is the actual niche

All six are visible from Google Maps in about a minute — which is precisely the skill already built at Social Hog, pointed at better economics.

Septic has a geography problem for the LA-in-person plan

Septic systems are suburban and rural — LA County is overwhelmingly sewered. The evidence is in our own lead list: of the 20 qualified leads, five are septic and none are in California (Nebraska, Maryland, Arkansas, Georgia). So septic cannot be the "drive around LA and meet owners" pool. Treat it as a nationwide remote test, run by phone, and keep the in-person motion on gates/fencing and later concrete/remodeling.

Do not open with these

Chiropractors, barbers, salons, restaurants, dentists, med spas. Low-ticket and heavily pitched — one incremental customer doesn't justify a real monthly fee. Already known territory from Social Hog; the point of BFMS is better economics, not the same pool.

HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing. Excellent economics, but among the most technology-forward home-service segments — already on Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, LSAs, automated quoting and agencies. "Website + Google + basic automation" isn't novel to them. Sell them later, with conversion case studies instead of "your website needs work."

Healthcare, legal, and other regulated businesses — specifically for the old-customer reactivation product. Consent, privacy and compliance create complexity you do not need during validation.

Before you ever dial

  1. Owner-operated and the owner is reachable
  2. Real reviews — visible proof people already pay them
  3. Meaningful job values — one job makes your fee feel small
  4. Visible evidence they're actually working — recent reviews, active listing
  5. A decision-maker you can get on the phone — no committee
  6. A gap you can point at before you speak — no website, or no way to request an estimate

The last one is what makes this not a cold call. You are never guessing — you are reporting something you can see.

2. The offer

It should sound almost boring. That's the feature, not a bug.

"You're clearly good at what you do, but your online presence doesn't reflect it. We'll fix the website and Google side, make it easy for people to call or contact you, track what comes in, and keep it maintained."

What you are NOT selling

AI. SEO. GEO. AEO. Funnels. Automations. Growth strategy. Digital transformation. Internally you use all of it. Externally you sell a visible fix. The moment you say "AI-powered" you become the fifteenth caller this week.

Two numbers in one breath, always. "Five hundred to get set up, then a hundred forty-nine a month." The monthly is part of the relationship from the first sentence — never an upsell you have to win twice. Client #1 became $400 with $0 recurring because that sentence came out wrong.

Why the monthly survives: we host it, so the monthly is the utility bill rather than a favor. And every site gets a tracking number (~$1.50/mo) — which proves the site works and, more importantly, surfaces the data that sells the second sale.

Higher-value systems only appear when their own data asks for them

Never invent a custom AI project because it sounds interesting. The monthly report exposes the next bottleneck, and then the system practically sells itself. Three upsells, in this order, one at a time:

  1. Old-customer / lead reactivation — they already earned this demand
  2. Missed-inquiry recovery — calls and form fills that went nowhere
  3. Estimate / quote follow-up — quotes sent, never chased

3. Where industries diverge

The industries are nearly identical at the first sale — same condition, same offer, same pitch. They diverge after it, and that's what eventually picks the niche.

Tree → missed calls and estimates, NOT a customer database

Correcting an earlier claim

An earlier draft called database reactivation the tree upsell. That's wrong: a tree removal is usually a one-off. There's no 3-year repurchase cycle to mine. Tree fits the website → calls → follow-up path, not the website → dormant database path. Pruning reminders, storm outreach and review generation are real but secondary.

What's genuinely true: their phones are busiest exactly when every crew is up a tree, and storms create sudden concentrated demand. Lawn and tree calls convert relatively well, so a missed call is measurable lost revenue.

"When that storm came through in March — how many calls came in that you couldn't get to? And did anybody follow up with the ones you didn't quote?"

Sell, in order: missed-call recovery → estimate follow-up → review generation → annual pruning / storm outreach.

Septic → database reactivation (the strongest play on this page)

EPA guidance: inspect every 1–3 years, pump every 3–5 years. A septic company that's been running fifteen years has thousands of homeowners who are due or overdue, and almost none of them have a disciplined reminder system.

"How many customers have you pumped over the years? ... So you've got somewhere around a thousand homeowners who are going to need you again, and no system bringing them back when they're due. Why is that not running automatically?"

Why it lands: it isn't an invented AI gimmick — it's their own earned demand, and the reminder genuinely helps the homeowner, because routine service is cheap and a replacement runs thousands to tens of thousands.

Caveat repeated from §1: septic is suburban/rural, so this is a remote, phone-run test — not part of the LA in-person motion.

The strongest sentence in the whole playbook — and it only works for septic: "Why are we paying to find strangers when hundreds of homeowners you already served are becoming due again?" If ten septic clients all react to that the same way, that's your niche, and the reactivation machine is the product.

Fencing / gates / ironwork → estimate follow-up

Quote-heavy sales cycle, and almost nobody chases the quotes that don't close.

"Roughly how many estimates do you send in a month? ... And what happens to the ones that don't come back?"

Sell: estimate follow-up. The math does the selling: "You quoted a hundred and eighty grand of work last month. What happened to the ones who never answered?"

Caveat: most people don't buy a second fence in three years, so the recurring economics here are less automatic than septic. Excellent for the first ten sales; earn the long-term verdict with the scoreboard. And it needs enough quote volume for follow-up to matter — ask before you sell it.

The discovery questions that find all three

  • "When everybody's in the field, what happens to the phone?"
  • "How many estimates do you send in a month, and who follows up?"
  • "How many customers have you served over the years — and do you ever reach back out?"
  • "What kind of work do you actually want more of?"

Ask these on the first call even though you're selling a website. You're not pitching yet — you're loading the next two years of revenue into your notes.

Templates already built

The vertical copy templates for tree service, septic and fencing are already written and committed — headlines, service lists, FAQs and schema per vertical. Your three priority verticals are covered. Run python -m src.website.build --business "Name, City ST" --vertical tree_service and you get copy, structured data, a paste-ready build brief, and a sendable mockup.

The sentence that makes you valuable again

Monthly delivery is automated so that your only human contribution is the part that actually matters:

"You got 22 calls last month and missed 9 of them."

"You sent 17 estimates and I can't see any follow-up on 11 of them."

That one sentence is the entire recurring relationship, and it's also how the second sale arrives without you pitching anything.

The failure mode to watch for

If you're still personally building every site at month six, you've bought yourself a freelance job with extra steps. The contractor hand-off at 3–5 clients is not optional — it's the difference between a business and a slightly better-paid version of what you're already doing.

6. Scripts by vertical

Same structure every time: name the proof, then name the gap. Never a generic script.

Tree — huge reviews, no site

"Hey, is this [Name]? I'll be straight with you, this is kind of a cold call — you can hang up if you want. Gimme twenty seconds and then tell me to get lost?"

"So — you've got a hundred and fifty-two five-star reviews. That's more than anybody else around there. And when I went to click through and learn more, there was nowhere to go. You're winning on the map and invisible everywhere else. Did you know it looked like that?"

Then stop talking. This frames it as finishing a job they already started, not starting a new one — which is why it converts better than the standard cold open.

Septic — lead with the calendar

"You've got eighty-two five-star reviews and no website, which is the first thing — but honestly the bigger thing is this. How many tanks have you pumped over the years? ... Right, so a big chunk of those are due again right now, and nothing's reminding them. Let's fix the website first, then I want to show you what that list is worth."

Gates — walk in, don't call

"Hey — are you the owner? I'm Miles, I'm local, over in [area]. I pulled up your Google before I came by — fifty-four five-star reviews and no website. So I went ahead and built you an example. Mind if I show you on my phone real quick?"

They get fifteen calls a day and zero visits. A human at the counter with something already built is unignorable.

The objections

"I get all my work by word of mouth." — "Honestly that's the best kind of customer there is, I'm not knocking it. But even your referrals Google you before they call, just to check you're real. Right now there's nothing to find, so you're losing the ones who weren't totally sure — and you never hear about it."

"I'm booked solid." — "Good problem. Then it's less about more jobs and more about better ones. Being easy to find lets you be pickier and charge more instead of taking whatever comes in."

"My nephew's building me one." — "Ha, that's how half of them start. Usually gets to eighty percent and stalls because he's busy with his own stuff. No shade. Difference is mine's done this week and somebody maintains it. If he beats me to it, genuinely great."

"Is there a monthly?" — "Yeah, a hundred forty-nine, cancel anytime, no contract — hosting, updates, your Google, reviews. You can buy it outright instead, but then when your hours change that's on you."

"I tried this before, it didn't work." — "Yeah, what happened? ... That's frustrating and pretty common. That's why you see it before you pay, and if it's not doing anything you cancel."

"How much?" (early) — "Way less than you'd think — five hundred to get going. Let me show you what I'd build first, then the number makes sense or it doesn't."

The close — stop selling the moment they say yes

"Cool — let's just do it. I'll text you a payment link right now, and while I've got you let me grab a few things so I can start today."

Don't hang up without: payment sent · exact business name · services in their words · hours and service area · photos · domain status · Google access request · a specific delivery date · the next call booked.

Payment: three Stripe links made once — $500 setup, $1,500 build, monthly subscription. Text it while they're on the phone. The monthly must be a real subscription; chasing invoices monthly will kill you faster than anything else here.

If it ever becomes a sellable asset

It won't be because you learned to crank out websites faster. It'll be because you accumulated distribution, relationships, standardized workflows, vertical knowledge, and eventually useful data. Those are the only things a buyer pays a real multiple for.

The one thing to keep in front of you: BFMS isn't here to prove you're impressive. It's here to prove you can commit, sell, create value, collect money, keep customers, manage people, and turn chaos into a system. UPath is where you aim those capabilities at something you actually care about. And the danger right now isn't picking the wrong vertical — it's spending another week researching which vertical is perfect. Your offer doesn't require that decision yet. Your next ten customers will make it for you.
