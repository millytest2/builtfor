OUTBOUND — how to actually talk to these people

For the 50 in the call sheet.

Two rules before anything else.

Run the check first. Every word below depends on you having looked. Without findings you are a stranger selling websites and you will sound like one.

Don't read this out loud. People can hear a script in about three seconds. What follows is the shape of a conversation and the one fact you need to land. Everything else is you, talking normally.

1. Never open with "website" or "Google"

Every shop on this list gets called about both most weeks. They say no in four seconds and they're right to. Say "I build websites" and you're the fourth one this month.

Open with the thing nobody else is calling about.

Never firstInstead
"I build websites for local businesses""I asked ChatGPT about your shop and it said it has no information about that business."
"I can fix your Google listing""I looked you up the way a customer would and found something weird."
"Do you have a website?"Save it. That's the disqualifier, after the finding lands.

The website and the listing are what you sell. Never what you open with.

2. The one thing to memorise

Not a script. A fact about their business, in your own words:

I searched [their trade] in [their town]. Google had them on page two.
ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude named other shops and not them. Then I asked
ChatGPT about them by name and it said it has no information about that
business. They have [21] five-star reviews.

That's it. That's the whole call. If you know those five facts cold you can have this conversation half asleep, and it'll sound like a conversation because it is one.

3. The five beats

Beats, not lines. Hit them in order, in whatever words come out.

1 · Admit it's cold. Three seconds. "I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call, give me twenty seconds and tell me to get lost." Honesty buys you the next thirty seconds because nobody else does it.

2 · Deliver the finding, then shut up. Facts first, compliment second, question last. Roughly: page two on Google, three assistants named other shops, asked about you by name and it said it doesn't know you, and you've got 21 five-star reviews so that seemed off. Then: "Did you know it looked like that?" And stop. Do not fill the silence. The next voice is theirs.

3 · Find out if they're real. "Has anyone ever built you a website, or just never got around to it?"

AnswerRead
"Had one, it was useless"Best lead you'll get. Paid once, got burned.
"Been meaning to"Live. Inertia, not refusal.
"My nephew's on it"Live. It stalled at 80%. It always does.
"Don't need it, I'm booked"Thank them, hang up. Don't spend twenty minutes.

4 · Ask one real question and shut up again. "When somebody who's never met you needs a bumper fixed, how do they usually find you?" Write down the answer. That answer is the whole rest of the relationship.

5 · Ask for ten minutes, offer two times. "I already wrote up what I found on one page. Let me send it, then call you back and walk you through it in ten minutes. Thursday morning, or is Friday better?"

Then get the email and one last thing: "what kind of work do you actually want more of?"

4. How to not sound like a robot

This is the part that matters more than the words.

  • Talk slower than feels right. Nerves speed you up. Slow is confident.
  • React before you continue. "Huh." "Okay, yeah." "That's fair." Real people respond. Scripts plough on.
  • If they interrupt, stop mid-sentence. Let them talk. Don't finish the thought you were on.
  • Use their words back. They say jobs, you say jobs. Don't upgrade it to "projects" or "clients."
  • Leave the silence. Three seconds feels like ten to you and normal to them. Most people fill it themselves, and what they fill it with is the good part.
  • Say you don't know when you don't. "I'd have to look" beats a guess and builds more trust than anything you could have made up.
  • Say their name once, at the start. More than twice is a telemarketer.
  • One idea per sentence, then stop. Stacking three benefits is the single clearest tell.
  • If you're smiling they can hear it. They also hear when you're reading.

Phrases that scream script

Cut these entirely.

"How are you doing today?"Nobody cold-calling cares and they know it.
"The reason for my call is..."Nobody talks like this.

6. Voicemail, then text. This is most of them.

The voicemail isn't the pitch. It's permission for the text. The text does the work.

Voicemail, thirty seconds, sound like a neighbour not a salesman:

Hey Mike, Miles here, I'm local over in Burbank. Looked up Alpine Auto Body
online before I called and found something you'd probably want to know about.
Nothing's wrong, nobody's in trouble. Two minutes when you get a sec,
310-606-9788. I'll text you what I found.

Text, within five minutes:

Miles here, just left you a voicemail. I asked ChatGPT about Alpine Auto Body
and it said it has no information about that business. You've got 21 five-star
reviews so that seemed off to me. Wrote up a page on it, want me to send it?

Lowercase, no punctuation gymnastics, no links in the first one. It should look like a text, because it is one.

7. Email

Same principle. A templated email gets deleted in the preview pane. Write like you typed it yourself, because you did.

Rules

  • Plain text. No logo, no HTML, no signature block with a graphic.
  • Lowercase subject after the first word. Capitalised subjects read as ads.
  • No greeting on its own line. "Mike," then a line break is fine. "Dear Mr Smith," is not.
  • Short paragraphs, one thought each. Three lines max.
  • One ask. Never two.
  • No attachment on the first email. Offer, don't push.
  • Reply in the same thread so it threads on their phone.

8. Walk in, if they're drivable

Twenty of the fifty are in LA. Better than calling and almost nobody does it.

"You the owner? I'm Miles, I'm local, over in Burbank. Looked you up before I
came by and found something odd. Can I show you on my phone? Takes a minute."

Turn the phone around. Same five beats from there. Sun Valley has a cluster and four auto body shops sit on one block of Branford Street, so that's one trip.

9. The second call, where money happens

Send the report 24 hours ahead. Have it open.

Recap in thirty seconds. "Did you get a chance to look?" Either answer's fine.

Walk their page, not your service. Three things: no website on the listing, the listing itself is mostly blank, and nothing online connects their trade to their town to their name. Then: "Your reviews are great. That part's already working. Everything else is just empty."

Scope it out loud before you name a number. "Two quick things so I price this right. Is your listing claimed, do you know? And how many services do you want people finding you for?"
