What actually sets the price you can charge

Not their revenue — what one customer is worth to them. A septic company selling $400 pump-outs feels $2,000 in their teeth. A remodeler selling $30,000 kitchens doesn't notice it. Both are equally easy to find with the same tool. That single reframe is the whole upgrade.

Where the new model is genuinely weaker — plan for it

  1. Prospecting gets harder for pure people-businesses (coaches, consultants) who have no map listing. Fix: prioritize premium categories that DO appear on Google Maps — remodelers, designers, photographers, event planners, tutoring centers, training studios. The machine still works on those.
  2. Scope creep. Message work has no natural end. Fix: the written "not included" list on page 3, said out loud on the call.
  3. Longer cycle. Fix: lean on warm and referral, where a $2k ask is normal.

3. The 7-Day Client-Booking Build

Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline. The fence is the product.

What's included

  1. Core site, up to 5 pages — who it's for, what they get, why them
  2. Message sharpening — one positioning pass, written up as a short doc they keep
  3. Proof organized — testimonials, results, portfolio, credentials, presented properly
  4. One booking or application flow — Calendly, Tally, or a form that actually routes
  5. Follow-up — one email or SMS sequence, three messages, so nobody goes cold
  6. Tracking — call tracking number plus booking/form events, so they can SEE leads
  7. Search basics — title/meta, LocalBusiness + FAQ schema, Google profile aligned

What's NOT included — say this on the call

  • No SEO retainer, no paid ads, no content calendar, no social posting
  • No logo or brand identity design
  • One booking flow, not three. One follow-up sequence, not a funnel.
  • Two rounds of revisions, then it ships
  • No custom integrations beyond the one booking tool
Define day one correctly: the seven days start when their materials arrive — photos, testimonials, offer details — not when they pay. Otherwise you are late on every single project because they didn't send their testimonials, and it looks like your fault.

4. Qualifying and pitching

Five checks before you spend a minute on them. All five, or move on.

The qualification checklist

  1. Visible proof people pay them — 10+ reviews at 4.5★, or a real portfolio/client list
  2. One customer worth $1,500+ — this is what decides what they can pay you
  3. The owner is the decision-maker — no committee, no marketing director
  4. No website, or no clear path to book — the gap has to be obvious in 10 seconds
  5. Reachable — a phone number that rings a human

The pitch — unchanged from the original, just aimed higher

"You're clearly good at what you do — I can see [22 five-star reviews / your portfolio / the results you're posting]. But when somebody looks you up, there's no clean place to understand what you offer, trust you, and actually book. I can build that for you this week."

That's the same move that worked for septic companies: name real proof, then name the gap. Nothing to argue with, no diagnosis required, and it flatters them on the way in.

The close

"It's fifteen hundred, and it's done in seven days from when you send me your photos and testimonials. Half now, half when it's live. I'll text you the link — want me to start this week?"

Half-now/half-on-delivery is the right structure at this price. It lowers the ask, proves you'll finish, and $750 is still real money in the door today.

Where to find premium leads

  • Your own networks first. Client #1 was a trainer from Daniel's network — this category IS your warm list. Free, and it converts.
  • Google Maps for the map-listed categories (remodeling, design, photo, events, tutoring, training) — the existing tool does this the moment the API key works.
  • Instagram / LinkedIn for the rest. A Linktree instead of a website is the qualification signal.
  • Associations: NKBA (kitchen/bath), ASID (interior design), PPA (photographers), IECA (admissions), ICF (coaches). Member directories full of real businesses with bad sites.
  • Channel partners: one wedding venue, gym owner, or realtor = many warm intros.
