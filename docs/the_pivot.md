# Critique of the last answer, and a better one

21 Sep 2026.

---

## Part 1: What was wrong with what I gave you

### 1. I opened a door you're not allowed to walk through

I told you to lead with *"I called twice and it went to voicemail."* Strong opener. But
then what do you **sell** him?

The fix for missed calls is missed-call text-back — which I told you four messages ago
**not to sell until you have 10 clients**, and which doesn't exist yet. It's a
simulation in this repo, not a working product with a Twilio number behind it.

So the prospect says *"okay, so how does a website answer my phone?"* and you have no
answer. **That's worse than the old opener**, because it creates an expectation gap
instead of just being boring.

### 2. Revealed preference cuts both ways, and I only applied it one way

This is the real one.

I told you a business with 100+ reviews and no website is a bad lead, because *"they've
had money and been pitched for years and didn't buy — that's a revealed preference, not
a gap."*

**I never applied the same logic to the no-website segment itself.**

> A tree service that has operated for 20 years without a website has revealed a
> preference. Against buying a website.

The entire targeting thesis in this repo — find businesses with no website — **selects
for people who have already demonstrated they don't want the product.** That's why the
survey data says their #1 reason is "not relevant to my industry." That's not an
objection to overcome. It's a stated preference, repeated at scale, for a decade.

I built forty leads on that filter and never questioned the filter. Your three nos are
the filter working exactly as designed.

### 3. The Ring Test has a downside I didn't flag

*"I called you twice yesterday"* can land as surveillance rather than diligence. Some
owners will hear it as "you called me twice and hung up?" And plenty of trades call back
within the hour or have an answering service, which kills the finding entirely. I
presented it as unambiguously strong. It isn't.

### 4. I kept giving you new metaphors instead of one answer

Local visibility utility. Then the front desk. Then the chain of six links. **Three
framings in three messages.** You asked five times because I kept producing a new one
rather than admitting the underlying problem was the target, not the description.

That's the exact failure the critique in this repo already names: building feels like
progress, and a new framing is the fastest thing to produce.

### 5. I told you to pivot to a product you don't have

Missed-call capture needs a Twilio number, call forwarding, an auto-responder, and a
support obligation — if it breaks, **their phone breaks.** That's a terrifying thing to
deliver as client #2. Recommending it as the wedge was bad advice.

## Part 2: The better answer

### Stop calling people who don't buy things. Call people who already buy this thing.

The hardest fact to establish about any prospect is **willingness to pay for
customers.** You've been filtering for the opposite.

**There is a segment that has already proven it, in writing, every month:**

> **Businesses paying Thumbtack, Angi or HomeAdvisor for leads.**

They are not debating whether customer acquisition is worth money. They settled that
question and they're writing the check. Your job stops being "convince him this
matters" and becomes **"here's a cheaper way to get the same thing."**

That's a switching pitch, not a new-purchase pitch. Enormously easier.

### What they're actually paying

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Thumbtack, per lead | **$20–$60** typical, $15–$150+ by trade |
| Angi, per lead | **$15–$85**, plus ~$300/yr membership, plus a $300–1,500 upfront deposit |
| Tree service leads specifically | **$25–$85 each** |
| **Shared with** | **3 to 8 competitors simultaneously** |
| Close rate | ~10–15% |
| **Real cost per booked job** | **$150–$400** |
| Leads that never reply at all | ~70–75% |

And it is genuinely, loudly hated. Contractor forums use the words "predatory lead
billing" and "unconscionable." **In January 2023 the FTC ordered HomeAdvisor (Angi
Leads) to pay up to $7.2 million** for misrepresenting how often leads convert.

### Now do the math out loud on a call

> "You're paying fifty bucks a lead, and they sell that same lead to four other guys.
> So you're really paying two, three hundred a job. I'll build you the thing that makes
> your own phone ring — five hundred once, then ninety-nine a month. **That's less than
> two leads.** And it's yours, and nobody else gets it."

**$99/month against $150–400 per booked job.** You don't have to sell that. You just have
to say it and stop talking.

### It also fixes the lead-sourcing problem

Here's what I could never do for the no-website list: **find them reliably.** A business
with no website cannot appear in search results, so I was inferring absence from silence
and getting it wrong.

**Thumbtack and Angi profiles are fully indexed.** I confirmed it. You can find these
businesses in thirty seconds, and their presence on the page *is* the qualification —
if they're listed as a pro, they're paying.

```
site:thumbtack.com tree service <city>
site:thumbtack.com septic <city>
site:angi.com/companylist tree service <state>
```

Open a profile: you get the business name, the city, the review count, how long they've
been in business, and usually a phone number. **No inference. No guessing.**

### The new opener — a question, not a finding

> "Hey, is this [Name]? Miles here, you don't know me — quick one and I'll let you go.
>
> Are you still running leads through Thumbtack?
>
> *(he will answer this — it's a shared grievance, not a pitch)*
>
> What are they charging you a lead these days?"

Then shut up. **Whatever number he says is the rest of the conversation**, and he said
it, not you.

> "Yeah. And they sell that same one to four other guys, right? … So what I do is build
> the version you own. Let me put one together for you, takes a day, costs you nothing
> — Thursday morning or Friday afternoon?"

This works because you are not asking him to believe anything. You asked a question, he
supplied the number, and the arithmetic is his own.

## Part 3: The honest risks in this pivot

I'm not going to sell you this the way I sold you the last one.

| Risk | Straight answer |
|---|---|
| **They already have websites** | Most will. You're no longer selling "you need a website," you're selling "stop renting leads." Different pitch, and it requires you to actually believe your site will outperform their Thumbtack spend. |
| **They're getting leads, so they may be content** | Some are. The disqualifier becomes *"is Thumbtack working out for you?"* A happy Thumbtack customer is a polite hang-up, same as booked-solid was. |
| **They've been pitched by agencies** | Yes, more than the septic guy in Hahira. You're a smaller number and a simpler promise, but it's a more contested room. |
| **You can't promise to replace their lead flow** | And you must not. The claim is *"cheaper, and you own it,"* not *"same volume." * Over-promise here and you own their revenue, which is the trap we've been avoiding all along. |
| **This is still unproven** | It is a better hypothesis than the last one, not a fact. Twenty dials tells you. |

## Part 4: What doesn't change

Most of it. This is a change of **who you call and how you open**, not a rebuild.

- **The offer stays:** $500 + $99/mo, first month included, $800 if they insist on owning
  it outright.
- **Delivery stays:** the same 14-day build, the same run sheet. **No new product
  required** — that's the main reason this beats the missed-call pivot.
- **The category stays, and I'm not renaming it again:** you're the front desk. Say "I
  build websites" on the phone.
- **The metric stays:** did they stay on the phone past twenty seconds.
- **The legal, Stripe and tracker setup stays** exactly as written.

### What to do next

1. **Keep your 40 leads but demote them.** Four of them have a listing marked CLOSED —
   those are still genuinely provable problems and worth calling.
2. **Build 40 Thumbtack/Angi leads** with the search operators. Twenty minutes.
3. **Twenty dials with the Thumbtack opener.** Log whether they stayed past twenty
   seconds.
4. **Then compare.** You'll have ~25 dials on "no website" and 20 on "stop renting
   leads," from the same guy on the same script. **That comparison is worth more than
   anything else you could do this week**, and nobody can hand it to you — not me, not
   ChatGPT, not Hormozi. It only exists after the dials.
