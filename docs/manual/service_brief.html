1 · What the service is

A local business is good at the work and invisible to the systems that now decide who gets called. Their Google listing is half empty or was never claimed. They have no website, or one built years ago that nothing links to. So when a customer searches their trade in their town, or asks an assistant who to call, somebody else gets the job.

We fix both halves as one job, because neither works alone. A website nothing points at is invisible. A listing with no site attached is a dead end. Then we keep it current and re-check every month whether it is working.

The category

We get local businesses found. It starts with a website. That is the category and the sequence. The outcome is what is sold; the website is the first deliverable, not the identity. "A website service" is a commodity that competes with Wix and somebody's nephew, so the opening line on a call is never "we build websites."

Not an SEO agency. Not a Google optimization agency. Not a marketing or content agency. Not an AI company. Not a receptionist or answering service. "Website" is the only word in this space a non-technical owner already understands and already believes they need, and it is a thing rather than an open-ended service, so the shape of the purchase is already familiar: you pay somebody, you get a website, there is a monthly for hosting.

Everything else is invisible work: listing claimed, structured data, service and town pages, consistent citations. Invisible work is miserable to sell and impossible to explain at a kitchen table, so it is never sold separately. It is what is inside the website.

AI goes behind the desk, never in front of the customer. No chatbot talks to the people calling them, and nothing replaces a person on the payroll. The point is more work coming in, not fewer staff needed.

Where the moat actually is

Not in the work. The work gets commoditized and self-serve versions are already shipping. The moat is that somebody does it for a person who will never do it themselves.

A sixteen-year-old starting something will use the software. A fifty-eight-year-old gate installer with 21 five-star reviews and a listing he has never logged into will not create an account, learn a dashboard, or do it on a Sunday. When self-serve competitors arrive they pull the DIY crowd out of the funnel at no cost to us, which sharpens this segment rather than shrinking it.

Price the person who drives out, not the task.

5 · The Visibility Check

The free door, and the entire pricing argument. Twenty minutes per prospect, nothing but a browser, and every input is public.

  1. Search their trade and town the way a customer would. Record where they land.
  2. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude the same shopping question. Record who got named.
  3. Then ask each about the business by name: "What can you tell me about [name] in [town]?" Record the answer word for word. "I don't have information about that business" is the sentence that sells the job.
  4. Pull up their Google listing. Note what is empty: website link, hours, categories, service areas, services, photos.
  5. Search their business name plus their main service. Note whether anything connects the two.
  6. One page: where they turn up, what is missing, and at least one thing that is already working.

Always include the thing that works, usually their reviews. It proves you looked and it keeps the page from reading as an attack on something the owner is proud of. Nothing is submitted on their behalf and nobody is called unless they ask.

7 · What this is really building

Two transferable skills, and they are the actual return on the time.

Sales. Cold conversations with strangers, at volume, at a price said out loud.

Ranking, in depth. How search engines and assistants index, read and cite a business. Why one gets named in an answer and another does not. Measured rather than guessed.

The second is the sleeper. The same mechanism that gets a gate installer named when someone asks for one points straight at getting any product cited when someone asks an assistant to recommend one. That is the same problem with a much bigger prize.

So instrument it. Every client, every month, log the exact queries, the Google position, and whether each assistant named them. One sheet across all clients. After thirty businesses that is a dataset almost nobody has.
