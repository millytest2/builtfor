1. The service ladder

One machine, four rungs. Each rung has a different buyer, a different price, and a specific trigger that moves them up.

Rung 0 — not a product, the door
The Free Report Card — $0
The audit. Graded A–F on their Google profile, reviews, and website. Costs nothing, takes one command, and opens every conversation on this page.
Rung 1 — cash this week
Starter Site — $500 + $149/mo
Any local business with no website. One call or one walk-in. 2–4 days to build. Light monthly: hosting, edits, Google upkeep.
Rung 2 — the revenue engine
Client-Booking Build — $1,500–2,500 + $349/mo
Owner-led businesses where one client is worth $1,500–25,000. Site, message, proof, booking flow, follow-up, tracking. 14 days, two phases.
Rung 3 — the asset
Growth Systems — +$200–400/mo each
Missed-call text-back, quote follow-up, database reactivation, review engine. Sold one at a time, only when the data proves they need it.
The one rule that makes the ladder work: never quote a site price on its own. Two numbers in one breath — "five hundred to get set up, then a hundred forty-nine a month" — so the monthly is part of the deal, not a thing you have to sell twice. Client #1 became $400 with $0 recurring because that sentence came out wrong.

The monthly is different per rung — this was wrong before

A gate shop that paid $500 for a site will not pay $349/mo, and pretending otherwise just loses the recurring entirely. $149/mo for Rung 1, $349/mo for Rung 2. Same work, different depth, priced to what the client can actually carry.

3. Rung 2 — Booking Build · $1,500–2,500

14 days, two phases. Seven was a promise that breaks on the first client who's slow with photos.

Why not 7 days

The build is ~12–15 hours, which fits in a week. Every dependency doesn't: intake answers, photos, testimonials, logo files, domain login, Google access, message approval, a revision round. Photos alone routinely take a week. Plus message work needs a night of thought, and four builds a month can't run as four non-overlapping sprints part-time. Agencies quote 6–12 weeks — 14 days still stuns people, and it lets you over-deliver instead of apologize.

PhaseDaysWhat lands
1 — Live & bookable1–7Message approved, core site live, one booking flow working. They can start using it.

What's included

  1. Core site, up to 5 pages — who it's for, what they get, why them
  2. Message sharpening — one positioning pass, written up as a doc they keep
  3. Proof organized — testimonials, results, portfolio, credentials, placed properly
  4. One booking or application flow — a real calendar, not a contact form
  5. Follow-up — one email or SMS sequence, three messages
  6. Tracking — call tracking number + booking/form events
  7. Search basics — title/meta, LocalBusiness + FAQ schema, Google profile aligned

Not included — say it out loud on the call

  • No SEO retainer, no paid ads, no content calendar, no social posting
  • No logo or brand identity design
  • One booking flow, not three. One sequence, not a funnel.
  • Two revision rounds, then it ships

Day one starts when their materials arrive, not when they pay. Say it on the call, put it in the invoice.

Why this rung is the actual business: a website is a portable artifact — they can take it and leave. An automation is wired into their operations; switching it off breaks something they now depend on. One is a purchase, the other is a dependency, and dependencies are what recurring revenue is made of.

Delivery cost is a few dollars a month (Twilio ~$1.50 + fractions of a cent per AI reply) against $200–400/mo. It runs itself and the margin is ~95%. The upsell sequence, per client

  1. Day 30: first monthly report. One line: "you missed 14 calls."
  2. Text the missed-call phone mockup. Say nothing else.
  3. They ask what it costs. Quote it. Done.
  4. Ninety days later, if quotes are their bottleneck, offer follow-up. One at a time.

5. The three pitches

Same structure every time — name real proof, then name the gap. What changes is which gap.

Pitch A — no website at all (most of List A)

"Hey, is this [Name]? I'll be straight with you, this is kind of a cold call — you can hang up if you want. Gimme twenty seconds and then tell me to get lost?"

"So it's me and my buddy, we build sites for [trade] guys. I looked you up before I called — you've got a five-star rating and no website at all. So if somebody Googles '[trade] near me' right now, they're finding the other guys. Did you know it was like that, or...?"

Then stop talking. Let the silence sit. That's where it becomes a conversation.

Pitch B — dominant on Maps, absent from the web (the sleepers)

For the ones with 50–150 reviews and no site. These are not invisible — they're winning on Google Maps and completely missing from the rest of the internet. Different problem, much easier sell, because they already believe in being found.

"You've got a hundred and fifty-two five-star reviews — that's more than anybody else around here. And when I clicked through to learn more, there was nowhere to go. You're winning on the map and invisible everywhere else. That's a half-finished job, and it's a cheap fix."

Flattering, factual, and it frames the purchase as finishing something they already built — not starting something new.

Pitch C — has a site, no way to book (all of List B)

"You're clearly good at this — I can see the results you're getting for students. But when a parent finds you, there's no clear way to understand your process or actually book a consult. They get a contact form and silence. I can fix that in two weeks."

The 30-second qualification test — use it on List B before calling

  1. Open their site on your phone. Fast? Readable?
  2. Can you tell in 10 seconds who it's for and what it costs?
  3. Real booking link — a calendar — or just a form?
  4. Is their proof visible, or buried?
  5. Is the owner the decision-maker? Solo or 2–3 people.

Two or more failures = live target. Passes all five = move on, you'll waste the call.

6. Objections & the close

The eight you'll actually hear, then how to take the money.

"I get all my work by word of mouth."
"Honestly that's the best kind of customer you can get — I'm not knocking it. But even your referrals Google you before they call, just to make sure you're real. Right now there's nothing to find. So you're losing the ones who weren't a hundred percent sold, and you never even know it happened."

"My nephew is going to build me one."
"Ha — honestly that's how half of them start. And it gets to about eighty percent and stalls because he's busy with his own stuff, right? No shade to him. Difference is mine's done this week and somebody's actually maintaining it. If he gets to it first, great, genuinely."

"Is there a monthly? I don't want another subscription."
"Totally get it. It's [149/349], cancel any time, no contract — hosting, updates, your Google, reviews. If you'd rather just buy it outright I'll do that, but then when your hours change that's on you. Most guys don't want the headache."

"I'm booked solid, I don't need more work."
"Good problem. Then it's less about more jobs and more about better ones — being easy to find lets you be pickier and charge more instead of taking whatever comes in."

"I tried this before and it didn't work."
"Yeah, what happened? ... That's frustrating, and pretty common. That's why you see the thing before you pay, and if it's not making a difference you cancel. No contract."

"I need to think about it."
"Course. What's the part you'd be thinking about — the money, or whether it'll actually work?" It's always one of those two. Now handle the real one.

"How much?" (asked early)
"Way less than you'd think — [five hundred / fifteen hundred]. Let me show you what I'd actually build first, then the number makes sense or it doesn't. Fair?"

"How do I know you're legit?"
"Fair question. It's literally me and my buddy, we're in LA. I'll text you my cell right now, and here's a site I built for a client."

The close — stop selling the moment they say yes

"Cool — let's just do it. I'll text you a payment link right now, and while I've got you let me grab a few things so I can start today."

Hesitant? "Half now, half when it's live. Either way works."
Real skeptic? "Let me build the real thing this week. You look at it, and if you like it you pay me then."

Nine things you can't hang up without

  1. Payment sent or scheduled
  2. Business name exactly as they want it shown
  3. Services to lead with — in their words
  4. Hours and service area
  5. Photos — "text me 10–15 pics" (biggest cause of delay)
  6. Domain — own one? login?
  7. Google access — "I'll send a request, hit accept"
  8. A specific delivery date
  9. The next call booked
Payment: three Stripe Payment Links, made once — $500 setup, $1,500 build, and the monthly subscription. Text the link while they're still on the phone; people who "pay later" often don't. Square or PayPal are fine for one-time fees, but the monthly must be a real subscription — chasing an invoice every month will kill you faster than anything else in this business.
