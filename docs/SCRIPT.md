# Sales script: phone and email

For any local business. Swap in their trade: "tree removal", "a mechanic", "a
plumber". Emails go out from hello@builtformainstreet.com.

**Call one gets a yes to seeing the free preview and a time for the callback. It
does not sell.** Call two walks through the preview and names one price. Most of the work is being curious and brief.

## Pocket version: friendly, then the problem, then honest

What worked on the first real call (Ahles, Sept 30). Printable: `output/Call_Script.pdf`.

**1. Open warm** (alternate A and B, note which)
> A: "Hey, is this [business]? ... Hey [name], it's Miles. What's going on, how we
> doing today?" ... "You guys staying busy?"
> B: "Hey [name], it's Miles. Quick one: I came across [business] and saw you've got
> some great reviews. Do most of your new customers find you through word of mouth?"

Your name in the first five seconds. No name on file: "Hey, it's Miles, who am I
talking with?" Recording: "Heads up, I record my calls so I don't miss anything."

**2. What you noticed**
> "So I was looking around [town] and saw you guys have some great reviews [or: saw
> you guys are pretty new], but no website. So when someone new looks you up, there's
> nowhere to send them. Were you guys planning on getting that set up?"

**3. Whatever they say:** "Makes sense." Busy: "Good. That's the best time to have
it, when you can pick the jobs you want."

**4. Be honest**
> Made one: "Yeah, I'll be honest with you, this is what I do. I build websites for
> [tree services], and I actually went ahead and made you one, because it's easier to
> show you than explain it. No cost to look. Want me to email it over right now?"
> Not yet: "...I build websites for [tree services]. I'd be happy to put one together
> for you, free to look at, and you decide if you like it. Want me to make you one?"

**5. Lock the next call**
> "What's the best email for you? ... Then I'll give you a quick call tomorrow to hear
> what you think. Morning or afternoon better?"

Preview ready: send it while you're still on the phone. "Did it come through?"

**Price, first ask:** "Totally fair. Take a look first, and if you like it, I'll give
you one straight price, and it won't change."
**Price, second ask:** the one number for the plan you'd pick, then stop talking.
"For a crew like yours, it's $800 to set up and $199 a month. No long contract. But
look first."

## Who you are on the call

You looked a business up, found a real problem, and you are telling the owner.
Same as telling a neighbor their sign is out.

- **Slow down.** Owners trust calm.
- **Curious, not clever.** The best line on any call is a question.
- **Honest about being new.** "I'm local, and I do this one business at a time."
- **Let them talk.** If you talk more than a third of the time, ask a question.
- **A "no" is a rep.** It goes on the scorecard like everything else.

## Rules

- Call 8am to 8pm **their** time, never Sunday. Tree crews: 8 to 10am or 3:30 to
  5:30pm. Auto shops: 10 to 11:30am or 2 to 4pm.
- Your cell is fine for now. It never goes in an email or anything written.
- **No texts** until client #10.
- If you record, say so right after your name: "Heads up, I record my calls so I
  don't miss anything." California requires it.
- Never quote a range. Never promise rankings, leads or revenue. Never open with
  "we build websites."
- Three tries per business, then Dead. "Don't call again" is permanent.

## Before you dial (60 seconds)

Ask ChatGPT two things and write down the answers:

> Who's good for [what they do] in [town]?

> Tell me about [business] in [town]. How do I contact them?

Open Google Maps: is there a Website button, and where does it go? **Only say what
you actually saw.**

## Call one: get the yes to the preview

Use the flow in the pocket version above. Stop after step 2 and listen; the owner's
answer to the curious question tells you where to go next. Write their answers down.
They go in the check and on the site. Tap **Check booked** and set the next-step date.

**If they ask the price on call one:** use the two-step answer in the pocket version.

## Call two: show the check, name one price

**Show.** "Here's what a stranger sees when they look for you." Read what ChatGPT
said about their name.

**Pick the plan before the call** from what you saw: a one-truck business with a few
services is Small, most businesses are Standard, a big crew with a wide area or two
locations is Bigger. The owner never has to count anything.

**Name one number, then stop talking.**

| You picked | Say |
|---|---|
| Small | "It's $600 to build, then $99 a month. That keeps it hosted, edited, and your Google listing right." |
| Standard (most) | "It's $800 to build, then $199 a month. That adds a monthly report on what Google and ChatGPT say about you, and review requests with a card at the counter." |
| Bigger | "It's $1,000 to build, then $300 a month. That adds follow-up on every new lead and a call with me every month." |

If they want less, build fewer pages, never a lower price: "If we keep it to the
basics, it's the $600 plan."

**Terms, plainly.**
> "We build and host the site. The domain is in your name. The monthly covers
> hosting, reasonable edits and your Google listing, plus what your plan adds. Cancel
> with 30 days' notice. After a year you can buy the site for $2,000."

**Close.**
> "If that works, I'll email the agreement and the payment link now. When the payment
> and your photos are in, the two weeks start."

**What we don't do.**
> "No ads, no social media, no chatbots, no long contract. We get you found, make you
> easy to reach, and show you the numbers every month."

## When they push back

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "How much?" | "Depends on what your business needs, so I won't guess. After the check, one number, and it doesn't move." |
| "Just send me something." | "Sure. Best email? I'll send the preview and call you tomorrow to hear what you think." |
| "Word of mouth keeps me busy." | "Good, that's the best customer there is. Those people still look you up before they call. Right now, when they do, [what you found]." |
| "I'm booked solid." | "Then it's about better jobs, not more. Which jobs do you want more of?" |
| "I have a Facebook page." | "That works for people who already follow you. When a stranger asks Google or ChatGPT, Facebook mostly gets skipped." |
| "My nephew can build it." | "He can. The listing, what Google and ChatGPT read, and checking it every month is the part that slips." |
| "I pay Angi / HomeAdvisor." | "Keep it if it works. This is the customers who search for you directly, so you're not paying per lead for them." |
| "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. We're at builtformainstreet.com. The check is free and you pay nothing unless you go ahead." |
| "Not interested." | "Understood. Is it timing, or you don't want more of this kind of work?" One try, then thank them. |
| "Don't call again." | "Understood, you're off the list." Mark Dead. |

## Voicemail (20 seconds)

> "Hi, this is Miles with Built for Main Street. I looked up [business] on Google and
> ChatGPT this morning, and there's something you'll want to see. The check is free.
> Call me back at [Voice number], or email hello at builtformainstreet dot com.
> Again, [number]. Thanks."

Tap Voicemail. If they have an email, send email 1 the same day.

## Emails, from hello@builtformainstreet.com

Every sales email ends with your mailing address and a way to opt out. The law
requires both. Send to yourself first.

**Email 1. Cold, or after a voicemail.**

> **Subject:** [Business] on Google
>
> Hi [Owner],
>
> I looked up [business] this morning the way a customer would. When I asked ChatGPT
> for [what you do] in [town], it named other companies, not you. [And there's no
> website for Google to send people to.]
>
> I'm local, in Los Angeles. I do a free 10-minute check that shows what customers
> see when they look for you and what's keeping you off the list. Want me to send it?
>
> Miles Tipton
> Built for Main Street · builtformainstreet.com · [Voice number]
> [mailing address]
> Reply "stop" and I won't email again.

**Email 2. Three days later, same thread.**

> Hi [Owner], quick follow-up on [business]. Happy to send the check, no cost. If now
> isn't the time, just say so and I'll leave it there. Miles

**Email 3. Check booked.**

> **Subject:** Your free check, [day and time]
>
> Hi [Owner], thanks for the time today. I'll call you [day] at [time] and walk you
> through what Google and ChatGPT show for [business]. Ten minutes. Talk then, Miles

**Email 4. After call two, when they say yes.**

> **Subject:** Built for Main Street, next steps for [business]
>
> Hi [Owner], thanks. Here's what we agreed: the [Small / Standard / Bigger] plan,
> $[setup] to build, then $[monthly] a month starting the day the site goes live.
>
> 1. The agreement: [link]
> 2. The payment link: [link]
> 3. Send 10 to 20 photos of your real work and your team.
>
> The two weeks start when the payment and photos are in. Photos are due within 30
> days. Miles
