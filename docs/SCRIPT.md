# Call script

The call sheet fills this in per shop (owner name, what ChatGPT said, the broken
link). This is the plain version to learn it from.

**Call one books the free check. It does not sell.** Call two shows the check and
names one price.

## Rules

- Dial by hand. Only 8am to 8pm **their** time. No Sundays.
  Best windows: 8 to 10am and 3:30 to 5:30pm their time.
- **No texts.** Not after a voicemail, not ever, until client #10 (TCPA).
- Call from a Google Voice number, never your own cell.
- Never quote a range. Never promise rankings, leads or revenue.
- Never open with "we build websites."
- Three tries per shop: a morning, an afternoon, then next week. Then Dead.
- "Don't call again" means Dead, permanently.

## Before you dial (60 seconds)

Ask ChatGPT about them by name:

> Tell me about [shop] in [town]. What do they do and how do I contact them?

Paste its answer into the call sheet. That line is your opener.

## Call one: book the check

**1. Open**
> "Hi, is this [owner]? This is Miles with Built for Main Street. I'll be honest,
> this is a cold call. Can I have 30 seconds to tell you why, and you can hang up
> after that?"

**2. What we saw**
> "I asked ChatGPT about [shop] by name this morning. Here's what it said: '[their
> words].' Did you know that?"

If the shop has a hook on the sheet, use it instead or add it:

| Hook | Say |
|---|---|
| Wrong link | "Your Google listing has a website link. It goes to [domain]. That's a different company." |
| Rented page | "The website on your listing is [x].localsearch.com. That page belongs to the directory, not you." |
| Empty domain | "You own [domain], but it still says the site is coming soon." |
| Name mix-up | "When people search your name, a different company comes up first." |
| Blank link | "A few directories show a website button for you with nothing behind it." |

Then **stop talking.** Silence does the work.

**3. One real question**
> "Where does most of your work come from right now?"
> "What kind of job do you want more of?"

Write the answer down. It goes in the check and on the site.

**4. The ask**
> "We run a free check on shops like yours: what Google and ChatGPT say when
> somebody looks for [trade] in [town], and what's keeping you off the list. Ten
> minutes on the phone. Tomorrow at 9 or at 4, your time?"

If they ask the price on call one:
> "It depends on how many services and towns, so I won't guess. After the check
> you get one number, and it doesn't move."

## Call two: the check and the price

**Show.** "Here's what a stranger sees when they look for you." Read their own name
answer out loud.

**Count.** "How many different services do you offer?" "How many towns will you
drive to?"

**Name one number.** Only the one they land in.

| They have | Say |
|---|---|
| Up to 4 services, 3 towns, one location | "It's $600 to build if you take the first year of upkeep up front, $1,990. That's the only way that price exists." |
| Up to 8 services, 6 towns | "It's $1,000 to build, then $199 a month. Cancel with 30 days' notice." |
| More than that | "From $1,500. I'll send you the exact number today." |

**Terms, plainly.**
> "We build and host the site. The domain is registered in your name. Upkeep covers
> hosting, reasonable edits, and a monthly report on what the AI assistants say
> about you. After a year you can buy the site outright for $2,000."

**Close.**
> "If that works, I'll send the agreement and the payment link now. When the payment
> and your photos are in, the two weeks start."

Money and photos first. Nothing gets built on spec. Photos are due within 30 days
of payment.

**What we don't do.**
> "No ads, no social media, no chatbots, no long contract. We get you found and make
> you easy to reach, and we show you the numbers every month."

## When they push back

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "How much?" | "Depends on services and towns, so I won't guess. After the check, one number, and it doesn't move." |
| "Just send me something." | "Sure. What's the best email? I'll send your check and call you in two days to walk through it." |
| "Word of mouth keeps me busy." | "Good. Those people still look you up before they call. Right now, when they do, [what we saw]." |
| "I have a Facebook page." | "That works for people who already follow you. When a stranger asks who to call, Facebook mostly gets skipped." |
| "My nephew can build it." | "He can. The site's the easy part. The listing, what Google and ChatGPT read, and checking it every month is the part that slips." |
| "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. Your number is on your Google listing. We're at builtformainstreet.com. The check is free and you pay nothing unless you go ahead." |
| "Too busy for more work." | "Then which jobs would you rather have? The check shows what comes up for those." |
| "Not interested." | "Understood. Is it the timing, or you don't want more of this kind of work?" One try, then thank them. |
| "Don't call again." | "Understood, you're off the list." Mark Dead. |

## Voicemail (20 seconds)

> "Hi, this is Miles with Built for Main Street. I looked up [shop] on Google and
> ChatGPT this morning, and there's something on your listing you'll want to see.
> The check is free. Call me back at [Google Voice number], or email hello at
> builtformainstreet dot com. Again, [number]. Thanks."

Then mark Voicemail. If the shop has an email, send the follow-up from the call
sheet. It includes a mailing address and a "reply stop" line, which CAN-SPAM
requires in any sales email. Never send a text.
