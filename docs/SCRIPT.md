# Call script

Written for independent auto repair shops, the niche we lead with. It works for any
local owner: swap the trade and the question.

**Call one books a free check. It does not sell.** Call two shows the check and
names one price. Most of the work is being curious and brief.

## Who you are on the call

You are not asking for a favor. You looked a shop up, found a real problem on its
listing, and you are telling the owner. It is the same as telling a neighbor their
sign is out.

- **Slow down.** Say less than you think you need. Owners trust calm.
- **Curious, not clever.** The best line on any call is a question.
- **Honest about being new.** "I'm local and I'm building this one shop at a time"
  lands better than sounding like an agency.
- **Let them talk.** If you are talking more than a third of the time, ask a question.
- **A "no" is a rep, not a verdict.** It goes on the scorecard like everything else.

## Rules

- Dial by hand. Only 8am to 8pm **their** time. No Sundays.
- Auto shops: call or walk in **10 to 11:30am or 2 to 4pm**. Never at drop-off
  (7:30 to 9) or pickup (4:30 to 6).
- **No texts.** Not after a voicemail, not ever, until client #10 (TCPA).
- Call from a Google Voice number, never your own cell.
- Never quote a range. Never promise rankings, leads or revenue.
- Never open with "we build websites."
- Three tries per shop: a morning, an afternoon, then next week. Then Dead.
- "Don't call again" means Dead, permanently.

## Before you dial (60 seconds)

Ask ChatGPT two things and write down what it says:

> Who's an honest mechanic in [town]?

> Tell me about [shop] in [town]. What do they do and how do I contact them?

If they are not in the first answer, or the second draws a blank, that is your
opener. Check their Google Maps listing too: no Website button, or a button that
goes to Facebook, Yelp or a dead page.

## Call one: book the check (five steps)

**1. Hello, and ask for 30 seconds**
> "Hi, is this [owner]? This is Miles with Built for Main Street. Honest heads-up,
> this is a cold call. Can I have 30 seconds, and you can hang up after that?"

**2. Say what you found, then stop**
> "I looked up [shop] this morning. When someone asks ChatGPT for an honest
> mechanic in [town], it names three other shops. Not you. And there's no website
> for Google to send people to. Did you know that?"

Then **stop talking**. Count to three in your head. Let them react.

**3. One real question**
> "Where do most of your new customers come from right now?"

Listen. Then:
> "What kind of job do you want more of? Brakes, diagnostics, the bigger stuff?"

Write the answers down. They go in the check and on the site.

**4. The ask**
> "Here's what I'd suggest. I run a free check on your shop: what Google and ChatGPT
> say when somebody looks for a mechanic in [town], and what's keeping you off the
> list. Ten minutes. I can call you, or I'm local and can stop by. Tomorrow at 10:30
> or at 2?"

**5. Lock it in**
> "Great. What's the best number and email to send it to?"

Set **Booked** on the call sheet with the time.

If they ask the price on call one:
> "It depends on how many services and towns you cover, so I won't guess. After the
> check you get one number, and it doesn't move."

## The walk-in (San Fernando Valley shops)

Go at 10 to 11:30am or 2 to 4pm. Have ChatGPT open on your phone.

> "Hi, is the owner around? ... I'm Miles, I'm local. I'm not selling anything today.
> I looked your shop up on the way over. Can I show you something? Thirty seconds."

Ask ChatGPT in front of them: "Who's an honest mechanic in [town]?" Hand them the
phone.

> "You're not on it. I do a free check that shows why and what fixes it. Can I bring
> it back Thursday morning?"

If the owner is busy: "No problem. When's a slow time?" Write it down and come back.

## Call two: show the check, name one price

**Show.** "Here's what a stranger sees when they look for you." Read out what
ChatGPT said about their name.

**Count.** "How many different services do you do? Brakes, A/C, diagnostics,
transmission, smog?" "Which towns do your customers come from?"

**Name one number.** Only the one they land in. Most auto shops land in Standard.

| They have | Say |
|---|---|
| Up to 4 services, 3 towns, one location | "It's $600 to build, then $99 a month. That keeps it hosted, edited and your Google listing right." |
| Up to 8 services, 6 towns | "It's $800 to build, then $199 a month. That adds a monthly report on what Google and ChatGPT say about you, and we ask your customers for reviews with a card at the counter." |
| More than that, or two locations | "It's $1,000 to build, then $300 a month. That adds follow-up on every lead that comes in, and a call with me every month." |

Then **stop talking** again.

If they want Small but have more services, do not drop the price. Drop services
from the site: "If we build it for four services, it's Small." The tier follows the
count, never the haggle.

**Terms, plainly.**
> "We build and host the site. The domain is in your name. The monthly covers
> hosting, reasonable edits and your Google listing, plus what your plan adds.
> Cancel with 30 days' notice. After a year you can buy the site outright for $2,000."

**Close.**
> "If that works, I'll send the agreement and the payment link now. When the payment
> and your photos are in, the two weeks start."

Money and photos first. Nothing gets built on spec. Photos are due within 30 days
of payment.

**What we don't do.**
> "No ads, no social media, no chatbots, no long contract. We get you found and make
> you easy to reach, and we show you the numbers every month."

## When they push back

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "How much?" | "Depends on services and towns, so I won't guess. After the check, one number, and it doesn't move." |
| "Just send me something." | "Sure. What's the best email? I'll send your check and call you Thursday to walk through it." |
| "Word of mouth keeps me busy." | "Good, that's the best customer there is. Those people still look you up before they come in. Right now, when they do, [what you found]." |
| "I have a Facebook page." | "That works for people who already follow you. When a stranger asks Google or ChatGPT who to call, Facebook mostly gets skipped." |
| "My nephew can build it." | "He can. The site's the easy part. The listing, what Google and ChatGPT read, and checking it every month is the part that slips." |
| "Is this a scam?" | "Fair question. Your number is on your Google listing. We're at builtformainstreet.com. The check is free and you pay nothing unless you go ahead." |
| "Too busy." | "Then which jobs would you rather have? The check shows what comes up for those." |
| "Not interested." | "Understood. Is it the timing, or you don't want more of this kind of work?" One try, then thank them. |
| "Don't call again." | "Understood, you're off the list." Mark Dead. |

## Voicemail (20 seconds)

> "Hi, this is Miles with Built for Main Street. I looked up [shop] on Google and
> ChatGPT this morning, and there's something on your listing you'll want to see.
> The check is free. Call me back at [Google Voice number], or email hello at
> builtformainstreet dot com. Again, [number]. Thanks."

Then mark Voicemail. If the shop has an email, send the follow-up from the call
sheet. It includes a mailing address and a "reply stop" line, which CAN-SPAM
requires in any sales email. Never send a text.

## After every call

Tap how it went on the call sheet. That is the scorecard. Nothing else to track.
