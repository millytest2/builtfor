# The state question, answered properly

You've asked three times, so let me stop saying "no" and show you the machinery
instead — then show you where the money you're actually reaching for is.

---

## Why Wyoming doesn't work, mechanically

States don't tax you based on where you filed paperwork. They tax you based on
**where the work happens.** California calls this "doing business," and you
meet the definition by being a California resident running the company from a
California address. Where the certificate was issued is not part of the test.

So a Wyoming LLC run by you from California means:

| | |
|---|---|
| Wyoming filing fee | $100 |
| Wyoming annual report | $60/yr |
| Wyoming registered agent (required, you're not there) | $50–150/yr |
| **California foreign LLC registration** (Form LLC-5) | $70 |
| California Statement of Information | $20 |
| **California $800 franchise tax** | $800/yr |
| **Total year one** | **~$1,100–1,200** |

Versus **$890** filing in California directly. You'd pay about **$300 more per
year for more paperwork in two states.**

And if you skip the California registration — which is what people actually
mean when they say "file in Wyoming" — the penalty is **$2,000 per year** for
each year you did business here unregistered, on top of the back taxes and
interest. You also can't sue anyone in a California court until you register,
which matters when a client stops paying.

**There is no version of this where you come out ahead.** It isn't a loophole
that's hard to find. It's a thing that doesn't exist, sold by companies that
make money selling registered agent subscriptions.

## Where the actual money is

The $800 is a **fee, not an income tax.** It doesn't scale, it isn't reduced by
anything, and at $120k of revenue it's **0.7% of your year.** You've been
hunting the smallest number on the page.

Here's what the same energy is worth aimed at the real levers. Run these on a
year where you're at $10k/mo — roughly **$120,000 revenue, ~$116,000 net** at
your margins.

### 1. QBI deduction — worth roughly $23,000 of deduction

Section 199A lets you deduct up to **20% of qualified business income**. It was
scheduled to expire at the end of 2025; the OBBBA made it **permanent** in July
2025, and the 2026 phase-out range runs $394,600–$544,600, so you're nowhere
near the limit.

On ~$116k net that's about **$23,000 off your taxable income**, worth roughly
**$5,000–7,000** in actual tax depending on your bracket.

**It applies in every state. It has nothing to do with where you file.**

### 2. S-Corp election — worth roughly $8,000/yr, later

This is the lever your coach was pointing at, and it's a federal election
(Form 2553), not a state one.

As a default LLC you pay **15.3% self-employment tax** on all net profit. As an
S-Corp you pay yourself a reasonable salary, pay SE tax on that, and take the
rest as distributions that aren't subject to it.

At ~$116k net with a $60k reasonable salary, roughly $56k moves out of SE tax.
**That's about $8,500/year.** Against $1,000–2,000/yr in payroll and an 1120-S
return, it nets somewhere around **$6,500–7,500**.

Which is why the threshold is around $50k of net profit, and why doing it today
at $0 revenue would cost you money.

### 3. Solo 401(k) — up to $72,000 deferred

Once profitable, this is the biggest single lever available to you. 2026 limits:

| | |
|---|---|
| Employee deferral | $24,500 |
| Employer contribution | up to 25% of compensation |
| **Combined maximum, under 50** | **$72,000** |

Open it at Fidelity or Schwab — **free**, no account fees. You don't need it
this year. Know it exists so you're not surprised by a tax bill in April 2028.

### 4. Ordinary deductions you're already spending

At a 97% margin your costs are small, which means almost everything you do
spend should be deducted:

Domains, hosting, Go High Level, software, the phone, a portion of your rent
and utilities if you have a dedicated workspace, mileage to client meetings,
the laptop, coaching fees, this business's share of your internet.

Track them from day one in the business account and it's automatic at tax time.

### The comparison, plainly

| Lever | Roughly worth | Depends on state? |
|---|---|---|
| QBI deduction | $5,000–7,000/yr | No |
| S-Corp election (above ~$50k net) | $6,500–7,500/yr | No |
| Solo 401(k) | Up to $72k deferred | No |
| Ordinary deductions | $1,000–3,000/yr | No |
| **Filing in Wyoming** | **−$300/yr** | — |

**The four things that matter are all federal.** None of them care where the
certificate was issued.

## The one state play that is real

It isn't where you *file*. It's where you *live*.

California's top marginal income tax rate is **13.3%**. Nevada, Texas,
Washington, Florida and Wyoming have **no state income tax**. On $116k of net
income, actually moving is worth something like **$7,000–9,000 a year** —
roughly ten times the $800 you've been trying to avoid.

But it requires genuinely relocating: moving your home, your driver's licence,
your voter registration, your bank, your time. California audits residency
claims aggressively and the burden of proof is on you. Filing a Wyoming LLC
from a Carpinteria apartment is not this and does not become this.

**You've moved several times already.** If a move is on the table for other
reasons anyway, the tax math is a real thumb on the scale. But it's a life
decision, not a paperwork one — and you shouldn't stall the business four
months waiting to decide.

## What to actually do

1. **File in California today.** $890 year one. Stop researching this.
2. **Deduct everything** through the business account from day one.
3. **Get a CPA at your first $3k month** — $400–800 for the year, and they'll
   find more than they cost.
4. **Elect S-Corp the January after you clear ~$50k net.** Retroactive if you
   file within roughly 2 months 15 days of the year starting.
5. **If you move states for life reasons, form there instead.** Don't move for
   the tax alone; do take the tax if you're moving anyway.

The $800 is the cost of one client's setup fee minus $200. You will have paid
for it in month one and never think about it again.
