# THE $10K REVISION

A critique of the current plan and the version that actually reaches $10k/mo.
Read `HANDOFF.md` first for context. This file supersedes its pricing and
targeting sections.

---

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

The build is not the problem and the plan is not stupid. The problem is that
the current offer sells the cheapest possible thing to the hardest possible
buyer, and the arithmetic of that combination never reaches $10,000 a month at
20 hours a week. You do not need a new business. You need to keep every tool in
this repo, raise the price by 4x, change the buyer from "no website" to "already
spending money and losing it," and move the website from the wedge to the
upsell. That is a repricing, not pivot number seven.

---

## 2. Why the current model cannot get there

### The client-count problem

| Offer | Clients needed for $10k/mo recurring |
|---|---|
| Starter Site, $149/mo | **67** |
| Booking Build, $349/mo | 29 |
| Blended realistic, $199/mo | 50 |

Sixty-seven small local clients is not a part-time cash engine. It is a
full-time support desk with churn. At 30 minutes per client per month, 67
clients is 33 hours a month of maintenance before a single sale is made, before
a single build is delivered, and before anyone answers a hosting complaint on a
Sunday. The model collapses under its own volume long before it pays $10k.

### The revealed-preference problem

`HANDOFF.md` correctly says a business with 152 reviews and no website has been
pitched a dozen times and declined, and that this is a revealed preference
rather than a gap. That logic does not stop at 100 reviews. A business with 34
reviews and no website in 2026 has also been pitched, repeatedly, by everyone
from GoDaddy to the last four cold callers this month. The entire no-website
pool is a graveyard of people who already said no to this exact offer. You are
not finding an unserved market. You are finding the people who are hardest to
convert, and then charging them the least.

The doc already contains the right answer and buries it as a footnote:

> "The best lead of all is one the tool can't currently find: a business that
> HAS a website and it's bad. Want is already proven."

That is not a footnote. That is the thesis.

### The geography problem

The proven channel is warm and local. The 20-lead call list spans Indiana,
Missouri, Tennessee, Georgia, Maryland, Texas, Oklahoma, Ohio and South Dakota.
Two leads are drivable from Los Angeles. Selling to nine states at once
destroys every advantage you actually have: you cannot walk in, cannot close in
person, cannot collect a referral from a neighbor, cannot build local density,
and cannot ever be the person who "did the Hernandez place down the road." You
built a national cold machine for a business whose only revenue came from
someone Daniel already knew.

### The skill-waste problem

You asked for a business combining AI, human judgment, sales, design and
delivery. A $500 five-page website expresses none of that. It is a commodity
with no AI leverage, no defensibility, and no room for design to matter. Your
strongest capability, the AI response systems, sits at Rung 3, sold last, to
people who already balked at the cheap thing. The most valuable asset you own
is buried at the bottom of the ladder.

### The anchor problem

You already learned this on client #1 and the doc states it well. Quoting "a
website for $400" makes every future recurring conversation an upsell fighting
your own anchor. The fix in the doc is to say two numbers in one breath. The
better fix is to stop leading with the website at all.

  later. Correct and disciplined.
- The 10-second disqualifier question. Saves hours.
- Two numbers in one breath. Never quote a setup fee alone.

- Fourteen days, not seven, because every dependency is on the client's side.
- Never saying "AI-powered" to the prospect.
- Section 11's self-critique. Most people never write that paragraph.

## 4. The three decisions

Everything below follows from these.

1. **Reprice.** Floor is $1,500 setup and $500/mo. Never sell below it again.
2. **Retarget.** Sell to businesses that already spend money on getting found
   and are losing the inquiries they paid for. Local, drivable, one job worth
   $5,000 or more.
3. **Re-sequence.** The AI response system becomes the wedge. The website
   becomes the upsell, sold at $3,500 after 60 days of data proves it is needed.

The third one is the important one. It inverts the whole ladder and it is the
reason the price can be 4x higher.

## 5. The offer

**You are not selling a website. You are selling the jobs they already paid for
and never got.**

A gate installer in Sun Valley spending $1,800/mo on Google Local Services ads
is buying inquiries. Some percentage of those inquiries call while he is holding
a welder, fill out a form at 9pm, or text and wait two days. Those are not lost
leads in the abstract. They are jobs worth $6,000 that he personally paid Google
to receive and then dropped. That is a bleeding wound with a dollar figure
attached, and he already believes in paying to fix it because he is already
paying.

Compare the two conversations:

| Current pitch | New pitch |
|---|---|
| "You don't have a website." | "I called you Tuesday at 2:14pm. Nobody answered and nobody called back." |
| Their reaction: I know, I don't want one. | Their reaction: wait, what? |
| You are cost. | You are recovered revenue. |
| Anchor: $500. | Anchor: one lost job, $6,000. |

### The ladder

| Rung | Offer | Price | Sold on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Leak Report** | $0 | The door. Evidence, not a pitch. |
| 1 | **Response Layer** | $1,500 + $500/mo | Every inquiry answered in 60 seconds, qualified, booked. |
| 2 | **Recovery Layer** | +$300/mo | Estimate follow-up and dormant reactivation. Sold on their own data. |
| 3 | **Site rebuild** | $3,500 one-time | Only after 60 days of data shows where the site loses people. |

Note what happened to design. It moved from a $500 commodity sold to a stranger
to a $3,500 project sold to a client who trusts you, has 60 days of evidence,
and is already paying you monthly. Same skill, seven times the price, no cold
call required.

## 6. The Leak Report

This replaces the free report card and it is the single best asset in the new
plan. It takes 20 minutes per prospect and it is impossible to argue with.

**How to produce one:**

1. Call their published number at a normal business hour. Log the exact time.
   Record whether it rang out, went to voicemail, or was answered.
2. If voicemail, leave one as a plausible customer. Start the clock.
3. Fill out their web form as a plausible customer with a real job description.
   Start the clock.
4. Text the number if it accepts texts. Start the clock.
5. Wait 48 hours. Log every response and every silence.
6. Screenshot the Google Local Services or Angi listing proving they pay for
   leads.
7. Put it on one page: what you did, when, and what came back.

**What it looks like on the page:**

> Tuesday 2:14pm, called. Rang 6 times, voicemail. No callback as of Thursday.
> Tuesday 2:19pm, submitted your contact form asking about a driveway gate.
> No response as of Thursday, 47 hours.
> You currently run Google Local Services ads in Sun Valley.
> A driveway gate is roughly $6,000. This happened twice this week that I know
> of, because I did it twice.

That page gets you the meeting. It is personal, specific, true, and about money
they already spent. It works on businesses that have websites, which is exactly
the pool `HANDOFF.md` identified as the best and the old tooling could not find.

**Automate it later, not now.** Do the first thirty by hand. The manual version
teaches you the objections and the honest version of the pitch. Build the
scraper after you have closed three.

## 7. Who to sell to

Same condition-niche discipline, new conditions. All six must be true.

1. **Drivable from Los Angeles.** Under 45 minutes. This is non-negotiable now.
   It restores the walk-in, the in-person close and the referral density.
2. **Already advertising.** Google Local Services, Angi, Thumbtack, Yelp ads, or
   Meta ads. They have proven they will pay to be found.
3. **One job worth $5,000 or more.** Gates and ironwork, ADU and remodel,
   hardscape and pools, custom fencing, tree removal, solar, restoration.
4. **Owner-operated, 2 to 15 employees.** Big enough to have inquiry volume,
   small enough that the owner decides in one conversation.
5. **They already have a website.** Intent proven. If it is bad, that is
   grievance plus intent, which is the best combination there is.
6. **A demonstrable leak.** You verified it yourself before you ever called.

**Drop the national list.** Indiana septic is a fine business and it is not your
business. The two LA gate companies in `HANDOFF.md` section 8 are the only two
leads on that list that fit the new model, and they fit it perfectly.

## 8. How to find them

The dead Places API is less of a blocker now, because the new signal is not
"absence of website." It is "presence of ad spend," and ad spend is public.

| Source | What it proves | How |
|---|---|---|
| Google Local Services ads | Paying per lead, right now | Search the category plus city, the LSA block is at the top |
| Meta Ad Library | Running paid social | Public, searchable, free |
| Yelp sponsored results | Paying Yelp | Visible on any category search |
| Angi and Thumbtack pro listings | Buying leads wholesale | Public directories |
| Google Maps, 10 to 49 reviews | Established, still hungry | The band analysis in HANDOFF was right |

This is a better finder than the old one and it needs no API key. Build it as a
scraper over ad surfaces once you have closed three by hand.

## 9. Delivery

All of it already exists in this repo or is a weekend of wiring.

- Twilio number, forwards to their real line, records and transcribes.
- Missed call fires an instant SMS from the AI, which qualifies and books.
- Web form submission gets an AI reply in under 60 seconds.
- Unclosed estimates get an 8-touch follow-up sequence.
- Owner gets an instant lead alert with the transcript and the job value.
- Monthly one-page report: inquiries received, answered by us, booked, and
  estimated dollar value recovered.

**That monthly report is the entire retention mechanism.** It is also the
upsell engine and the case-study factory. It structurally fixes the
$0-recurring problem, because cancelling means giving up a number they can see.
This is the answer to the client #1 mistake, built into the product instead of
bolted on as a conversation.

### Capacity check

At 15 clients, with 2 founders at 20 hours a week each:

| Line | Hours/month |
|---|---|
| Support, 30 min per client | 7.5 |
| Monthly reports, 15 min each | 3.8 |

That is 5.4 hours a week of delivery against 40 hours of capacity. Roughly 35
hours a week remain for selling. The model fits. The 67-client model does not.

## 10. Channels, and the honest funnel

`HANDOFF.md` ranks referrals and warm network first and second on real evidence,
then spends its energy on a cold call list. Fix that inversion.

**The cold math, for a $1,500 plus $500/mo product sold to a stranger:**

| Stage | Count |
|---|---|
| Dials | 100 |
| Conversations with the decision maker | 25 |
| Booked meetings | 8 |
| Showed up | 3.4 |
| Closed | 1.0 |

Roughly **99 dials per close**. Fifteen clients on cold alone is about 1,480
dials, which at 40 dials a week is 37 weeks. Cold cannot be the primary channel.
It is a floor, not a plan.

**Therefore, in priority order:**

1. **Client #1 debt.** Testimonial, before and after screenshots, live URL, case
   study, referral ask, and the monthly offer. Free, owed, and undone.
2. **The warm list.** Every business owner you and Daniel know, written down.
   Still never done. Still the only channel that has produced revenue.
3. **Three channel partners.** A general contractor, a commercial insurance
   agent, a supply house counter rep. One relationship yields repeated warm
   intros. This is the biggest unexploited lever in the whole document.
4. **Walk-ins, Leak Report in hand.** They get 15 calls a day and zero visits.
5. **Cold calls.** Volume filler. Never the strategy.

## 11. Timeline, honestly

You asked for $10,000 a month in 2026. There are about 13 weeks left in the
year, you have $0 recurring, and you have never quoted a price to a stranger.

**What is actually achievable:**

| Milestone | When |
|---|---|
| First stranger pays | 4 to 6 weeks |
| 8 clients, ~$5,400/mo recurring | Dec 31, 2026 |
| A single $8k to $10k month including setup fees | December 2026, possible |
| $10k/mo sustained recurring, 15 clients | Q2 2027 |

A $10,000 month in 2026 is reachable if you stack setup fees. A $10,000 per
month run rate is not, at 20 hours a week, from a standing start, and anyone
telling you otherwise is selling you something. The recurring base is what makes
it a business rather than a treadmill, and a recurring base takes about three
quarters to assemble at this pace.

**If you want $10k/mo sooner, there are exactly two levers:**

- Go full time. Roughly halves the timeline.
- Go bigger per client. Five clients at $2,000/mo instead of fifteen at $650.
  That means multi-location operators, franchise groups, or lead-gen companies.
  Longer sales cycle, harder proof burden, far fewer relationships to manage.
  Worth testing once you have three case studies, not before.

## 13. Critique of this document

Three things above are wrong or thin. Correcting them here rather than leaving
them to be discovered on a live call.

### The Leak Report method was partly unethical

Section 6 said to submit a form and call "as a plausible customer." Two problems.
Posing as a customer through a channel they pay per lead on, Google Local
Services or Angi, charges them real money, often $75 to $150, to receive a fake
inquiry. That is not a demonstration, it is taking money out of the pocket of
someone you are about to ask to trust you. And doing it at volume is deceptive
regardless of cost.

**The corrected method, which proves the same thing:**

- Call the **published** number only, never a paid ad number.
- Use their **own website form**, never a paid lead channel.
- Leave a real voicemail as yourself, with your real name and number, asking
  about a real job you would actually pay for.
- Disclose who you are the moment anyone responds.
- Time the callback.

A contractor who never calls back a named human who left a number has the exact
problem you are selling against, and now you can say so with clean hands. The
proof is identical. The method is defensible when they ask how you got it, and
they will ask.

### "AI receptionist" is a crowded category in 2026

Every GoHighLevel reseller sells missed-call text-back. The technology is not the
moat and never was. What is defensible: you are local and will drive out, you did
the work before asking for anything, and you can show a specific document about
their specific business. Sell the document and the person, never the stack.

### Churn is underestimated

Contractors cut discretionary spend the month work slows. At 15 clients, 4%
monthly churn means replacing roughly seven a year just to stand still. The
monthly recovered-revenue page is the whole defense, so it has to show a real
number and go out on time every month, even when the number is bad. Especially
when it is bad.

## 14. The service category

Get this right, because it decides who you are compared against and therefore
what you can charge.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category internally** | Lead response and recovery for local trades |
| **Category to the customer** | "We answer the calls you miss" |
| **Never say** | AI, automation, SEO, funnel, growth, digital transformation |
| **Compared against** | An answering service, a receptionist, a missed job |
| **Not compared against** | Web designers, marketing agencies, software |

The last two rows are the point of the exercise. A website priced at $500 is
compared against Wix and your nephew. Answering the phone is compared against a
part-time receptionist at $2,800 a month, and against the $6,400 job that went to
somebody else. Same work, same tooling, different shelf. The shelf is worth
about 4x.

The landing page in `site/` is built on this positioning: the hero is a document
about their business, not a feature list, and the free Leak Report is the door.
