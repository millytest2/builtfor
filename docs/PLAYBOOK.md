## 1. The niche: independent auto repair, San Fernando Valley first

**One niche for the coach: independent auto repair shops.** Start with the 19 in the
Valley, then the 4 in Amarillo and Wichita.

Why this one, from what we found rather than from feel:

| Test | Auto repair | Tree service | Training studios | Tutoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leads with no website, strict check | 11 of 20, then 11 of 25 more Valley shops | 8 of 20; every Amarillo and Lubbock crew now has a site | 11 of 20 | about 1 in 5 |
| Can you reach the owner? | At the counter all day. Walk in. | On a job. 7 to 8am or 4 to 6pm their time only | Between classes | Afternoons only |
| Fits your hours (after 3pm, Saturday morning) | Yes. Walk in 3:15 to 4:15, Saturdays 9 to noon | Central and Eastern crews, yes: their 5 to 7pm | Saturday only: their best hours are during your shift | Saturday only |
| Which tier they land in | Standard or Bigger. Brakes, A/C, diagnostics, transmission, smog, tires | Standard | Small | Small or Standard |
| Do customers come back? | For years | Once or twice a year | Monthly members | A school year |
| Can you show the problem live? | Yes. "Honest mechanic in Van Nuys?" on ChatGPT, in front of them | By phone only | By phone only | By phone only |
| Do you understand the customer? | Every driver fears being ripped off | Somewhat | Yes, your world | Yes |

The deciding points: the owner is reachable, the problem shows in 30 seconds on your
phone, and one brake job pays the month.

The others stay, in a different role:

- **Training studios**: warm referrals through the client #1 world. Not a cold list.
- **Tree service**: the 8 clean crews stay on the call sheet for Central-time afternoons.
- **Tutoring**: parked. Most independents already have a site.

**What to tell the coach.**

> "One niche: independent auto repair shops, starting in the San Fernando Valley,
> where I can walk in. Why: the owner is at the counter, I can show the problem on my
> phone in 30 seconds, and one brake job pays for a month of the service. What I've
> tested: about 20 sales of Google visibility work at Social Hog, and client #1 (a
> personal trainer, $400). With this exact offer, zero calls so far. That starts this
> week. Lead list: 50 leads with no website found, 23 of them auto shops."

**Pressure-tested.** The full table is in `docs/OFFER.md`:
- The most common average repair order at independent shops is $500 to $749, so $199
  is under half of one job.
- Auto-shop marketing firms charge $300 to $1,499 a month, so we're the cheap
  option.
- The real risks are owners who say they're "busy enough", Spanish-first counters,
  and lead supply. Each has a plan.

**Kill criteria after 25 owner conversations:**
- Fewer than 3 checks booked: change the opener.
- No sale after 8 offers: fix the offer, don't discount.
- Owners unreachable: walk-ins only.

This is the last niche change until 25 real conversations are logged.

## 2. The offer

> **Built for Main Street helps local businesses get found, get contacted, and stop
> losing good leads.**

**The door is free: the Visibility Check.** Ten minutes. What Google and ChatGPT say
when someone looks for a mechanic in their town, what they say about the shop by
name, and what is keeping it off the list. If everything already works, we say so.

**The build** is the same in every tier. It's done two weeks after the money and
photos arrive:

- A page for every service and every town
- The Google listing claimed in the owner's name and completed
- Tap to call on every page, and a quote form tested before launch
- The markup search engines and AI assistants read
- For California repair shops: the BAR-registered name, ARD number and phone on every
  page. BAR has required this in all online repair ads since October 1, 2025

**The monthly** depends on size. We count services and towns on the call, and the
count picks the price.

| Tier | Fits | Setup | Monthly | The monthly adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | Up to 4 services, 3 towns, one location | $600 | $99 | Hosting, reasonable edits, Google listing kept current, a visibility check every quarter |
| **Standard** | Up to 8 services, 6 towns | **$800** | **$199** | Small, plus a monthly report (search and ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude), review requests by counter QR card and email, review reply drafts the owner approves |
| Bigger | 9+ services, 7+ towns, or two locations | $1,000 | $300 | Standard, plus every lead routed to the owner and staff by email with a same-day follow-up draft, past-customer emails, a 15-minute monthly call |

**A typical auto shop** has brakes, A/C, diagnostics and check engine, oil and
maintenance, transmission, suspension, tires, and smog. That's 8 services, drawing
customers from about 5 towns. It lands in **Standard: $800, then $199 a month**.

**Terms**:
- Cancel with 30 days' notice.
- We own the site; the client owns the domain.
- Buyout at $2,000 after 12 months.
- Edits are reasonable, not unlimited.
- Photos are due within 30 days of payment.
- Money and photos come first; nothing gets built on spec.

**What we don't do**: ads, social media, chatbots, AI receptionists, SEO retainers,
long contracts. We never text before client #10. We never promise rankings, leads or
revenue.

## 3. How this helps an owner

A driver in Van Nuys has a grinding noise. She asks her phone, "Who's an honest
mechanic near me?" The phone names three shops. A shop with 20 years and good
reviews isn't one of them, because nothing online tells Google or ChatGPT what it
does or where. She never hears of it.

After the build:
- The shop has a page for brakes, a page for Van Nuys, and a Google listing that is
  complete and in the owner's name.
- The phone number taps to call.
- Every customer at the counter is asked for a review.
- Every month the owner gets one page showing whether Google and ChatGPT now name
  the shop, with screenshots.

What the owner gets: the customers who already look for a shop like theirs can find
it and reach it. We measure both every month. A typical brake job covers the $199.

## 5. The path to $10k a month

**The math**:
- Average client: about $820 to set up and $209 a month (70% Standard, 20% Bigger,
  10% Small).
- Assumes 3% of clients cancel each month.

| New clients a month | $10k a month by | Clients then |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | month 10 | about 33 |
| 6 | month 6 | about 28 |

**What it takes, per 100 phone touches.** These are assumptions until the scorecard
replaces them:

| Owner conversations | Checks booked | Checks held | Offers | Sales |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 6 | 4 | 3 to 4 | about 1 |

**Walk-ins** should run about 3 times better: an owner at the counter, and the
problem on your phone.

**The weekly target** comes to about 5 new clients a month, which is $10k around
month 7:
- 75 phone touches (15 a weekday, 3:15 to 5pm Pacific)
- 8 Saturday walk-ins

**The floor on a bad day**: 5 touches. Log them. A bad day still counts.

**When to call whom** (your clock, after your shift):
- **3:15 to 4:15**: Valley auto shops, by walk-in or phone. They're past the lunch
  rush and before the 4:30 pickup.
- **3:15 to 5**: tree crews in Central time (5:15 to 7pm theirs) and Eastern time
  (before 4pm yours). Crews answer after the last job.
- **Saturday 7 to 10am**: Central and Mountain auto shops and gyms. That's 9 to noon
  their time.
- **Saturday 9 to noon**: walk into Valley shops. Most are open Saturday mornings.
- **Gyms and tutors** have their best hours during your shift. Call them on a lunch
  break or Saturday, or skip them. The call sheet's "Call now" filter shows who is
  in a window at any moment.

**The second bottleneck is lead supply.** 75 touches a week at three tries per lead
uses about 25 new leads a week. At about 1 in 2 qualifying, that means checking 50
shops a week by hand. Adding `GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY` in the environment settings
lets the lead finder do that part.
