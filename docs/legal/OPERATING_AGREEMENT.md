# OPERATING AGREEMENT
## Built for Main Street LLC
### A Single-Member California Limited Liability Company

> **Fill in every `[BRACKET]` before signing. Delete this box.**
> This is a standard single-member operating agreement. No state files it — you
> sign it, date it, and keep it. The bank will ask to see it, and it is the
> document that proves the LLC is a real separate entity rather than you with
> extra steps. Have a lawyer read it once you are past ten clients; $300 then
> is cheap insurance, $300 today is not.

---

This Operating Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into and effective as of
**[DATE]** by **[YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME]** ("Member"), the sole member of
**Built for Main Street LLC**, a California limited liability company (the
"Company").

## 1. Formation

**1.1 Formation.** The Company was formed on **[FILING DATE]** by filing
Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) with the California Secretary of State,
entity number **[ENTITY NUMBER]**, under the Beverly-Killea Limited Liability
Company Act and the California Revised Uniform Limited Liability Company Act
(Corporations Code § 17701.01 et seq.).

**1.2 Name.** The business of the Company shall be conducted under the name
**Built for Main Street LLC**, or such other name as the Member may designate.

**1.3 Principal Office.** The principal office is **[STREET ADDRESS, CITY, CA,
ZIP]**, or such other place as the Member may designate.

**1.4 Registered Agent.** The agent for service of process is **[AGENT NAME]**
at **[AGENT ADDRESS]**.

**1.5 Term.** The Company began on the filing date and continues perpetually
until dissolved under Section 8.

**1.6 Purpose.** To engage in any lawful business, including without limitation
the design, development, hosting, licensing and maintenance of websites and
related online-visibility services for business clients.

## 2. Member and Capital

**2.1 Sole Member.** The Member owns one hundred percent (100%) of the
membership interests of the Company.

**2.2 Initial Capital Contribution.** The Member contributed **$[AMOUNT]** in
cash to the Company on **[DATE]**. *(Write a real number, even $100, and
actually transfer it from your personal account to the business account. A
documented funding transaction is one of the facts that makes the entity look
real if it is ever questioned.)*

**2.3 Additional Contributions.** The Member is not obligated to make further
contributions but may do so at any time. Any additional contribution shall be
recorded in the Company's books.

**2.4 No Interest on Capital.** No interest accrues on any capital contribution.

**2.5 Limited Liability.** The Member shall not be personally liable for any
debt, obligation or liability of the Company solely by reason of being a
member, except as required by law.

## 3. Management

**3.1 Member-Managed.** The Company is managed by its Member. The Member has
full and exclusive authority to manage the business and affairs of the Company
and to bind the Company.

**3.2 Authority.** Without limitation, the Member may: open and operate bank
accounts; enter into, perform and terminate contracts; acquire, license and
dispose of property including domain names, software and intellectual
property; borrow money; hire and terminate employees and contractors; retain
professionals; and take any other lawful action in furtherance of the
Company's purpose.

**3.3 Officer Title.** The Member may execute documents on behalf of the
Company under the title "Member," "Managing Member," or "Owner."

**3.4 Standard of Care.** The Member shall act in good faith and in a manner
reasonably believed to be in the best interests of the Company.

## 4. Separateness of the Company

*(This section is the point of the whole document. Follow it literally.)*

**4.1 Separate Entity.** The Company is a legal entity separate and distinct
from the Member. The Member shall preserve that separateness at all times.

**4.2 Separate Accounts.** The Company shall maintain bank accounts in its own
name, separate from any personal account of the Member. **Company funds shall
not be commingled with personal funds.** All revenue of the business shall be
deposited to a Company account, and all business expenses shall be paid from a
Company account.

**4.3 Distributions, Not Withdrawals.** Money moved from the Company to the
Member shall be recorded as a distribution or as reimbursement of a documented
business expense, and shall be recorded in the Company's books at the time it
is made. The Member shall not pay personal expenses directly from a Company
account.

**4.4 Books and Records.** The Company shall keep accurate books and records,
including a record of all contributions, distributions, income and expenses,
and copies of its formation documents, this Agreement, and its tax filings.

**4.5 Contracting in the Company's Name.** All client agreements, vendor
accounts, domain registrations and leases shall be entered into in the name of
the Company, not the Member individually, and shall be signed by the Member in
a representative capacity.

**4.6 Capitalization.** The Member shall keep the Company funded adequately for
its ordinary obligations.

## 5. Distributions and Taxes

**5.1 Distributions.** The Member may take distributions of available cash at
any time, provided the Company remains able to pay its debts as they come due.

**5.2 Tax Classification.** The Company is a single-member LLC and is by
default a disregarded entity for federal income tax purposes, with its
activity reported on the Member's individual return. The Member may elect a
different classification, including an S corporation election under IRS Form
2553, at any time.

**5.3 California Obligations.** The Company acknowledges the California annual
minimum franchise tax (FTB Form 3522, currently $800), any applicable LLC fee
based on total income (FTB Form 3536), the annual return (FTB Form 568), and
the Statement of Information (Form LLC-12, due within 90 days of formation and
biennially thereafter).

**5.4 Tax Distributions.** The Company may distribute to the Member amounts
sufficient to pay taxes attributable to the Company's income.

## 6. Intellectual Property

**6.1 Company Ownership.** All intellectual property created by or for the
Company in the course of its business — including website code, templates,
designs, content, domain names and client-facing materials — is the property
of the Company.

**6.2 Leased Assets.** Where the Company licenses or leases a website, domain
or related asset to a client rather than transferring ownership, the Company
retains title to that asset and the client's rights are limited to those
stated in the applicable client agreement.

**6.3 Assignment of Prior Work.** The Member assigns to the Company all right,
title and interest in any work product created by the Member before the
formation date that is used in the Company's business.

## 7. Transfer of Interest

**7.1 Transfer.** The Member may transfer all or part of the membership
interest at any time.

**7.2 Admission of New Members.** A new member may be admitted only with the
written consent of the Member, and this Agreement shall be amended in writing
to reflect the admission, the new member's contribution, and the allocation of
profits, losses and voting rights.

## 8. Dissolution

**8.1 Events.** The Company dissolves upon the written election of the Member,
or as otherwise required by law.

**8.2 Winding Up.** On dissolution, the Company's assets shall be applied
first to creditors, then to the Member. The Member shall file a Certificate of
Cancellation (Form LLC-4/7) with the California Secretary of State.

## 9. Indemnification

The Company shall indemnify the Member against claims, liabilities and
expenses arising from acts performed on behalf of the Company, to the fullest
extent permitted by California law, except for acts constituting fraud, gross
negligence, or willful misconduct.

## 10. General

**10.1 Governing Law.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of
California.

**10.2 Amendment.** This Agreement may be amended only in a writing signed by
the Member.

**10.3 Entire Agreement.** This Agreement is the entire agreement regarding
the operation of the Company and supersedes any prior understanding.

**10.4 Severability.** If any provision is held unenforceable, the remainder
of this Agreement remains in effect.

**10.5 Conflict with Law.** Where this Agreement conflicts with a
non-waivable provision of California law, that law controls.

### After you sign

1. Save a signed PDF. Keep it with the stamped Articles and the EIN letter.
2. Bring it to the bank appointment — most banks ask for it.
3. Make the initial capital contribution from Section 2.2 as a real transfer
   the day the account opens, and label it "initial capital contribution."
4. Re-read Section 4 once a month for the first three months. Commingling is
   the single most common way a single-member LLC loses its protection, and it
   almost never happens on purpose — it happens because a client paid a
   personal Venmo once.
