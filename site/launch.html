The money rule, from the first dollar: business money moves through the business account. Client pays you → business account. Never personal Venmo, Zelle or Cash App. You need money → transfer it and call it a draw. Start this habit as a sole proprietor and the LLC inherits it.

Only three things take your money for this business: the state at bizfileonline.sos.ca.gov, the county at lavote.gov, and a newspaper for the $27–28 publication. Anything else asking for a card to file something is selling you the ability to type your own name into a form. Filing mills buy ads against searches like “file LLC California” and run landing pages with the state seal, a navy header and a title like “US STATE FILING APPLICATION” — then charge $149–250 plus a “state fee” marked up above the real $70. Three tells: the domain ends in .com, not .gov; the URL contains something like /ads-landing/; and the quoted state fee isn’t exactly $70. The IRS is the same — an EIN is free at irs.gov/ein and nobody legitimate charges for one.
Don’t pay a formation service to do this. A real LegalZoom cart, priced out: Pro $249 + expedited $99 + the $70 state fee + $20 Statement of Information = $438 today. Then the line reading “billed annually after your 10 day trial” — the only line with no price shown — auto-charges $299/yr on day 11. Add the two $800 payments that filing in September triggers and it is $2,337 by April 15. The same outcome on your own timeline is $110 today, $109 in December, $800 in April 2027: $1,019. What the $438 buys is a 20-minute form, an operating agreement template you already have a better version of, an EIN service for something the IRS gives away, and $99 to arrive faster at a date that costs you $800. No formation service will tell you to wait until December 17 — they get paid when you file today. If you want the convenience and the address privacy, Northwest at $39 is the one worth paying for; it undercuts buying a registered agent on its own, and LegalZoom’s agent renews at $249/yr against Northwest’s $125. “It’s tax-deductible” is not a discount — a $438 deduction returns maybe $130, next April, against profit you don’t have yet.
Working remote doesn't change the California piece. States tax the person doing the work, not where the customers are. You're in California, so California has you — even though all 50 leads are in Indiana, Tennessee, Texas, Kansas and Ohio. The good news is the reverse: serving out-of-state clients does not create tax obligations in their states. Economic nexus thresholds run $100k+ of sales into a single state, and website services are generally not sales-taxable. So one state to answer to, not fifty. If you ever physically relocate, the business moves with you and California drops off — but that takes actually moving, not a mailbox.
Why the LLC waits for December. California's 15-day rule: an entity formed on or after December 17 that conducts no business that year owes no franchise tax for it. File today and you owe $800 for 2026 and $800 for 2027 within four months. File December 17 and you owe one $800, in April 2027. A sole proprietorship is a real business in the meantime — it takes money, signs contracts, holds a bank account. What it lacks is a liability shield, and the exposure here is small: no client premises, no equipment. The cap in your client agreement carries that load until December.

Everything else was checked and doesn't work. An LLC that elects S-corp status still owes the $800 — the first-year exemption belongs to entities incorporated as corporations, not to LLCs that choose corporate taxation. No legislation has reinstated the LLC waiver. There is no low-income or hardship exemption. California's veteran waiver covers selling goods, not services. New Mexico ($50, no annual report) and Wyoming ($100/$60) are genuinely the cheapest states, but working from California means registering here as a foreign LLC and paying the $800 anyway, plus theirs.

Incorporating is the one real alternative: $125 year one with $0 franchise tax. But a corporation owes Form 1120-S or 1120 every year even at zero income — $800–1,200 from a CPA — so over two years it runs ~$2,500 against the LLC's ~$1,030. Cheaper in year one, worse in every year after.
