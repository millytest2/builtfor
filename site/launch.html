The money rule, from the first dollar: business money moves through the business account. Client pays you → business account. Never personal Venmo, Zelle or Cash App. You need money → transfer it and call it a draw. Start this habit as a sole proprietor and the LLC inherits it.

Why the LLC waits for December. California's 15-day rule: an entity formed on or after December 17 that conducts no business that year owes no franchise tax for it. File today and you owe $800 for 2026 and $800 for 2027 within four months. File December 17 and you owe one $800, in April 2027. A sole proprietorship is a real business in the meantime — it takes money, signs contracts, holds a bank account. What it lacks is a liability shield, and the exposure here is small: no client premises, no equipment. The cap in your client agreement carries that load until December.

The one alternative: California corporations are exempt from the $800 in their first year, so incorporating costs $125 against the LLC's $890. But an S-corp owes Form 1120-S every year even at zero income — $800–1,200 from a CPA, forever — which makes it cheaper in year one and more expensive in every year after.
