Questions we get.

What exactly is the $79 for?

Hosting, your domain name, the security certificate, and keeping the Google listing current. The site has to live on a server somewhere and somebody has to pay for that. It is not a retainer, you are not buying our time, and there is no campaign to report on. Think of it like the phone line.

Can I cancel the monthly?

Any time, no notice. We hand you the files and the login for your domain. The Google listing was always in your name, so nothing changes there. We would rather you leave cleanly than feel stuck.

Do I actually own the website?

Yes. The domain is registered in your business name, the listing is verified to you, and the files are yours on request. Nothing is held hostage, which is more than you can say for most of this industry.

Why is it a range and not one price?

Because the work isn't the same for everyone. If your Google listing is already sorted there's less to do, and if you want a page for every service in every town you cover there's more. We scope it on the call, tell you the number, and it doesn't move after that. Most shops land at eight hundred.

Is there a cheaper option?

Seven hundred on the usual job if you get us your photos inside a week of saying yes. Photos are the thing that holds up every build, so that discount buys us speed and it is the only one we do.

How long until I see something?

Two weeks to live, starting the day your photos arrive. Turning up in search takes longer, usually four to eight weeks, because Google and the assistants have to re-crawl and re-index. We re-run your check at day 45 and every month after so you can watch it move rather than take our word for it.

What if it doesn't work?

Then you still own a website and a properly filled out Google listing for well under a thousand dollars, which is less than most people charge for either one on its own. And because we re-run the check monthly, you will know either way rather than wondering.

Will you run my ads or post for me?

No. We do not do ads, social media, blog posts, logos or rebrands. Plenty of good people sell those. We do one thing so we can be good at it.

Do you work outside Los Angeles?

Yes. The work is all remote except the part where we sit at your kitchen table, and plenty of clients skip that. If you are in the Valley we will come out.

What the machines actually read.

None of this is mysterious. An assistant answering "who should I call" is assembling from a handful of public sources, and a business missing all of them is not ranked low. It is absent.

Source 1
Your Google Business ProfileHours, categories, service areas, services, photos, and whether a website is linked. Most of the shops we check have three of those eight filled in.
Source 2
Your reviewsCount, rating, recency, and what people actually wrote. Usually the one thing already working.
Source 3
Structured data on your siteThe machine-readable version of your details and services. Invisible to people, decisive for assistants.
Source 4
Pages that name the work and the placeSomething that connects "driveway gates" to "Sun Valley" to your business name. Without it there is nothing to match on.
Source 5
Consistent listings elsewhereSame name, address and phone across the web. Contradictions make everything trust you less.
