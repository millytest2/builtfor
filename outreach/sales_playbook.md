# Sales Playbook — call, objections, close, payment

Everything from "hello" to "money received and work started." Read once, then keep
the objections section open while you dial.

---

## 0. The mindset that makes this sound human

The energy doesn't come from being excited about *your service*. It comes from being
genuinely interested in *their business* — and from having actually found something.

- **You are not asking for a favor.** You found a real problem and you can fix it.
  That's a peer conversation, not a beg.
- **Be excited about the FIND, not the sale.** "Dude, you've got five stars and you're
  completely invisible — that's such an easy fix" is genuine. "I'd love to help you grow
  your business" is salesman noise.
- **Curiosity beats pitching.** The person who asks the most questions controls the call
  and sounds the most human.
- **You're allowed to be a person.** Contractions, half-sentences, laughing, "uh," saying
  "honestly." Polished = salesman = guard up.
- **Never be attached to the outcome.** There are 700 more on the list. That indifference
  is what makes you sound confident instead of hungry.

**Tonality rules:** slow down · drop your pitch at the end of sentences (statements, not
questions) · smile on the opener, they hear it · use their name twice max · **after you
name the problem, shut up and let it sit.**

---

## 1. Openers

### A. WARM (network, referral, intro) — our highest-converting channel by far
No script needed. Just be normal and get to it fast.

> "Hey — random one. You know I've been building websites and doing the Google stuff for
> local businesses? I looked up [their business / your buddy's shop] and honestly you guys
> are killing it on reviews but there's basically nothing online. Want me to just make you
> something and show you? No charge to look."

**If it's a referral, always lead with the name:**
> "Hey, [Name]? Dave over at [Business] gave me your number — said you might be the guy to
> talk to. He mentioned you've been meaning to get a website sorted?"

### B. COLD — honesty as pattern interrupt
> "Hey, is this [Name]? ... [Name], I'll be straight with you, this is kind of a cold
> call. [light, smiling] You can hang up on me if you want. Gimme twenty seconds and then
> tell me to get lost?"

*(Almost everyone says "sure.")*

> "Appreciate it. So it's me and my buddy, we build websites for [trade] guys. I was
> actually looking you up before I called — you've got a **five-star rating** and, uh,
> no website at all. So if somebody Googles '[trade] in [town]' right now, they're not
> finding you. They're finding the other guys. Did you know it was like that, or...?"

**Then stop talking.** Let them answer. This is where the call becomes human.

### C. WORK-FIRST (text/email, no call) — the differentiator
> "Hey [Name] — I build sites for [trade] companies around [town]. Noticed you didn't have
> one so I went ahead and made you an example: [link]. No charge, no catch, it's yours to
> look at. If you like it I'll make it real. If not, no worries at all. — Miles"

## 2. Discovery — the part that makes you sound like a human, not a vendor

Ask 3–4 of these. Actually listen. Take notes — you'll use their words in the close.

- "How long you been doing this?"
- "How do people find you right now — mostly word of mouth?"
- "What kind of work do you actually *want* more of?" *(gold — this becomes the headline)*
- "When someone calls and you're up a tree / on a job — what happens to that call?"
- "You ever have people say they had trouble finding you online?"
- "Busy season coming up?"

**The missed-call question is the most valuable one you can ask.** Their answer sets up
your biggest add-on later, and it makes them realize the problem themselves.

---

## 3. The transition — from problem to offer

Don't pivot hard. Summarize what *they* said:

> "Okay so — you've been doing this eighteen years, you're five stars, and you're getting
> everything from word of mouth. That's actually the perfect setup, because the hard part's
> already done. You're good at the work and people like you. You're just invisible to
> anybody who doesn't already know you."

Then:

> "Here's what I'd do..."

---

## 4. The offer — one breath, two numbers, never a website alone

> "It's four hundred to get you set up — that's the site, your Google profile fixed up,
> click-to-call so it rings your cell, all of it. Then it's three forty-nine a month and we
> keep the whole thing running — updates, your Google, getting you reviews. **First month's
> included**, so nothing else comes out until day thirty. Sound fair?"

**Never say "a website for $400."** That creates a one-time sale in their head and every
recurring conversation after becomes an upsell fighting your own anchor.

If they want to buy the site alone:
> "Yeah I can do that — six hundred, you own it, you're on your own after. Honestly most
> guys go the other way since it's cheaper to start and we actually keep it working. But
> either's fine with me."

*(The plan costs less upfront. Let them notice that themselves.)*

## 5. Objections — all of them

**"How much?" (asked early, before value)**
> "Depends what you need, but way less than you'd think — four to six hundred to get going.
> Let me show you what I'd actually build first, then the number will make sense or it
> won't. Fair?"

**"Is there a monthly? I don't want another subscription."**
> "Totally get it. Yeah — three forty-nine, and you can cancel any time, no contract. It
> covers hosting, updates, your Google profile, reviews. If you'd rather just buy the site
> outright I'll do that for six hundred, but then when your hours change or you want a photo
> swapped, that's on you. Most guys don't want that headache."

**"I get all my work by word of mouth." (the #1 objection)**
> "Honestly that's the best kind of customer you can get — I'm not knocking it at all. But
> here's the thing: even your referrals Google you before they call. They just want to make
> sure you're real. Right now there's nothing for them to find. So you're losing the ones
> who weren't a hundred percent sold yet — and you never even know it happened."

**"I'm booked solid / I don't need more work."**
> "That's a good problem. Then honestly it's less about more jobs and more about *better*
> ones — showing up online lets you be pickier and charge more, instead of taking whatever
> comes in. And when you do have a slow month, it's already there working."

**"I already have a website."**
> "Nice — is it actually bringing you calls? ... [if no] Yeah, that's the difference. A site
> that just sits there isn't the same as one built to make the phone ring. Two minutes, no
> charge, let me show you what I mean."

**"My nephew/cousin/kid is going to build me one." (very common)**
> "Ha — honestly, that's how half of them start. And it usually gets to about eighty percent
> and then stalls out because they're busy with their own stuff, right? [laugh] No shade to
> him. The difference is mine's done this week and somebody's actually maintaining it. If he
> gets to it first, great, genuinely."

**"I tried this before and it didn't work."**
> "Yeah, what happened? ... [listen — this is real information]. That's frustrating, and
> honestly pretty common. That's why I do it different — you see the thing before you pay,
> and if it's not making a difference you cancel, no contract. I'd rather earn it monthly."

**"I'm too busy / not interested."**
> "No worries at all, I'll be quick — want me to just text you what I found so you can look
> when you've got a sec? Worst case you delete it."

**"Send me some info / email me."**
> "Sure — what's the best email? ... And real quick so I'm not sending you junk: is it more
> that you want more jobs, or more that you want to look legit when people check you out?"
> *(gets you back into a conversation)*

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Course. What's the part you'd be thinking about — the money, or whether it'll actually
> work?" *(then handle the real objection, which is one of those two)*

**"I need to talk to my wife/partner."**
> "Makes sense. Want me to send the example so you've got something to show her instead of
> just describing it? I'll check back Thursday."

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**
> "I can't really go under four — that's already pretty lean for what's in it. What I *can*
> do is start you on the single-page version and add pages later as you need them. That
> keep it workable?"

**"How do I know you're legit? / Is this AI? / Is this a scam?"**
> "Ha, fair question. No — it's literally me and my buddy, we're in [city]. I'll text you
> my cell right now, and here's a site I built for a client: [link]. You can call me
> anytime, you'll get me."

**"What's the contract? Am I locked in?"**
> "No contract. Month to month, cancel whenever. If I'm not earning it you shouldn't be
> paying it."

**"Who are you / where are you based?"**
> "I'm Miles, I'm in [city]. It's me and my buddy Daniel — we're small on purpose, that's
> why you get us on the phone instead of a call center."

**Voicemail:**
> "Hey [Name], it's Miles — I build websites for [trade] guys around [town]. Noticed you've
> got great reviews but no site, so your competitors are getting the Google traffic. Not a
> sales thing, I'll just text you an example. 310-606-9788."

## 6. The close

Once they've said anything that sounds like yes ("yeah that sounds good," "how would that
work," "what do you need from me") — **stop selling and start onboarding.** The number one
mistake is continuing to pitch past the yes.

**Assumptive close:**
> "Cool — let's just do it. I'll text you a payment link right now for the four hundred, and
> while I've got you let me grab a few things so I can start today."

**If they hesitate on paying now:**
> "No problem — I can do two hundred to start and two hundred when you see it live. Either
> way works."

**If they need one more step:**
> "Tell you what — let me build the real thing this week. You look at it, and if you like it
> you pay me then. If you don't, I've lost an afternoon, no hard feelings." *(Use sparingly,
> but it closes skeptics and you can reuse the build.)*

### Don't hang up without these
1. **Payment sent or scheduled**
2. **Business name exactly as they want it shown**
3. **Services they want to lead with** (use their words from discovery)
4. **Hours** and service area
5. **Photos** — "text me 10–15 pics of your trucks and jobs" *(biggest delay if skipped)*
6. **Domain** — do they own one? Login?
7. **Google access** — "I'll send a request to manage your Google listing, just hit accept"
8. **Delivery date** — name a specific day
9. **The next call booked** — "I'll call you Thursday to walk through it"

## 7. Payment mechanics

**Use Stripe Payment Links.** No code, works from your phone, text it mid-call.

Set up two links once, reuse forever:
- **Setup** — $400 one-time
- **Growth Plan** — $349/mo recurring subscription, start date +30 days

On the call: text the setup link while they're still on the phone. People who "pay later"
often don't. Getting the $400 while they're warm is the single biggest close-rate lever.

Alternatives if Stripe is a hassle: Square Invoices, PayPal, or Venmo/Zelle for the setup
fee — **but the monthly must be a real subscription** you don't have to chase. Chasing
invoices every month will kill you faster than anything else in this business.

**Deposit option:** $200 now / $200 on delivery. Use it to close a hesitant buyer.

---

## 8. After the yes — the first 72 hours

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Same day | Send receipt + "here's what happens next" text. Create their client YAML. Run the BEFORE audit. |
| Day 1–2 | Build the site from the generated brief. Request Google profile access. |
| Day 3–4 | Send the live preview link. **Ask for changes** — people need to feel ownership. |
| Day 5 | Go live. Send the AFTER audit next to the BEFORE. This is the moment that earns the retainer. |
| Day 5 | **Ask for the referral.** "Who else do you know around here who needs this?" Highest-yield question in the whole business — right after they're happy. |
| Day 30 | First monthly report. First invoice. Mention the missed-call number you're seeing. |

---

## 9. If they say no

They're not dead, they're just not today.
- Text the mockup anyway. "No worries — here's that example if you ever want it."
- Tag them for **90 days**, especially before their busy season.
- Ask one thing: **"Who else around here should I be talking to?"** A no that produces a
  referral is worth more than a maybe.
