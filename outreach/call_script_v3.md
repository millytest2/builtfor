# The Call Script — matched to the Growth Plan offer

Use with `docs/the_20.csv`. Every lead there has a verified gap in the
`gap_verified` column. **That column is your first twenty seconds.** Never dial
without one.

**The goal of the call is a booked ten minutes, not a sale.**

---

## Before you dial: the Ring Test (90 seconds, do it every time)

Call their business line twice, at two different times, and hang up when it picks up
or goes to voicemail. Write the result in the `ring_test` column: `2/2`, `1/2`, `0/2`.

Roughly a third of calls to small trades go unanswered. If they missed yours, that is
not a gotcha, it is the single best opening line available to you, and nobody else
does it.

---

## 1. Open

**If they missed your test calls (0/2 or 1/2):**

> "Hey, is this [Name]? I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call, you can hang
> up on me. Before you do — I actually tried you Tuesday afternoon and again this
> morning, and both times it went to voicemail. I'm not giving you a hard time, you
> were working. Can I tell you why I noticed? Twenty seconds."

**If they answered both times (2/2):**

> "Hey, is this [Name]? Straight up, this is a cold call, you can hang up. I'll say
> this though, I called you twice this week and you picked up both times, which already
> puts you ahead of most guys I call. Gimme twenty seconds?"

*Slow down. Statements, not questions. Smile — they hear it. Use their name twice, max.*

---

## 2. Disqualify, immediately

> "Quick one before anything else — have you ever had a website, or just never got
> around to it?"

| They say | What it means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| "Had one, it was useless" | **Best lead you'll get.** Want is proven, and someone let them down. | Keep going, hard |
| "Been meaning to" | Live lead. Inertia, not refusal. | Keep going |
| "That's it right there" *(points at Wix/Facebook)* | Live lead. They think they have one. | Go to opener C |
| **"Don't need one, I'm booked solid"** | **Hang up politely.** | "Fair enough, good problem to have. Take care." |

Do not burn twenty minutes on a no. There are forty more names.

---

## 3. The observation — then SHUT UP

Pick the one that matches `gap_verified`. Say it, then stop talking and let it sit.

### A. Free subdomain (Wix / GoDaddy / Weebly)
> "So I looked you up before I called. You've got [43] five-star reviews, people
> clearly like the work. But your website's on a free Wix address — the one that's got
> *wixsite* sitting in the middle of it. So somebody put real effort into building
> that, and then Wix put their own name on your front door. Did you know that's what
> customers see?"

**For the ones who never renamed the default** (A&W Septic, 845TreePro, Timberland):
> "...and the address actually just ends in the word *website*. That's the placeholder
> it ships with. Looks like it got started and then real work got in the way, which,
> honestly, fair."

**For A1 Septic specifically — the URL is misspelled:**
> "...and here's the thing nobody's told you. The address has a typo in it. It says
> s-o-l-u-t-o-i-n. Been that way a while. You want me to just tell you what I'd do?"

### B. No website at all
> "So I looked you up first. [Sixteen] five-star reviews, twenty years in — you're
> clearly good at this. And when I went to click through and actually learn more,
> there was nowhere to go. You're doing great on Google Maps and you're basically
> invisible everywhere else. Did you know it looked like that?"

### C. Facebook only, plus a duplicate listing marked CLOSED
*(Collins Septic, Heavenly Gates — this is the strongest opener on the whole list)*
> "So before I called I looked you up, and I found something you'll want to know about
> regardless of whether we ever talk again. You've got two listings out there, and one
> of them says **CLOSED**. So some of the people searching for you right now are seeing
> that you went out of business. ... Yeah. I can tell you exactly how to fix that, takes
> about ten minutes and it's free, I'm not charging you for that."

*That one earns the rest of the call outright. Give the fix away.*

## 4. One discovery question — write the answer down verbatim

This is the Revenue Recovery sale in sixty days. Do not skip it.

| Vertical | Ask |
|---|---|
| Tree | *"When everybody's out on a job, what happens to the phone?"* |
| Septic | *"How many tanks have you pumped over the years?"* *(the reactivation list)* |
| Fencing / gates | *"How many estimates go out a month, and who chases the ones that don't come back?"* |
| **Anyone on Thumbtack / Angi / Houzz** | ***"What are you paying per lead on Thumbtack right now?"*** |

That last one is the most valuable question in this script. Most of these businesses
are already buying leads at $50–100 each, shared with two competitors. Their answer is
the whole price justification and they said it, not you.

---

## 5. The transition

> "Okay so — you've been at this [twenty-two] years, you're five stars, and you're
> getting most of it word of mouth. That's actually the perfect setup, because the hard
> part is already done. You're good at the work and people like you. You're just
> invisible to anybody who doesn't already know you."

---

## 6. The offer — two numbers, one breath, never the site alone

> "It's **four hundred to get you set up** — that's the site, your Google profile fixed
> up, a tracking number so you can actually hear the calls it brings in, click-to-call
> so it rings your cell. Then it's **three forty-nine a month** and we keep the whole
> thing running. **First month's included**, so nothing else comes out until day thirty.
> You can cancel any time and the site's yours either way. Sound fair?"

**If they ask to just buy it outright:**
> "Yeah, I can do that — **six hundred**, you own it, you're on your own after. Honestly
> most guys go the other way, since it's cheaper to start and we actually keep it
> working. Either's fine with me."

*The $600 exists to make the $400 + $349 look like the smart choice. It is not a
downsell you push. Let them pick it and mean it.*

---

## 7. The booking close — this is the entire call

> "Here's all I'd actually suggest. Let me build you a real example first. Takes me a
> day, costs you nothing, and if you hate it you've lost nothing. Then I'll walk you
> through it in ten minutes. **You around Thursday morning, or is Friday afternoon
> better?**"

Why it works: the appointment is to look at **their own thing**, it's **ten minutes**,
and you offered **two specific times** instead of "when works for you."

**Lock it:**
> "Perfect, Thursday at nine. This the best number? And real quick so the example isn't
> generic — what kind of work do you actually *want* more of?"

**Text within five minutes:**
> "Miles — good talking to you. Thursday 9am, I'll have that example ready.
> 310-606-9788"

## 8. Objections — every one routes back to the booking

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"How much?"** | "Four hundred to get going, three forty-nine a month after, first month included. But let me show you the actual thing first, then the number makes sense or it doesn't. **Thursday or Friday?**" |
| **"That monthly's steep"** | "What are you paying Thumbtack per lead right now? … So this is about four leads' worth a month, except you own it, and it doesn't get sent to two other guys at the same time." |
| **"Is there a contract?"** | "No. Month to month, cancel whenever. If I'm not earning it you shouldn't be paying it." |
| **"Word of mouth is all I need"** | "Best customers there are, genuinely. But even your referrals Google you before they call, just to check you're real. Right now there's nothing to find. You're losing the ones who weren't a hundred percent sure — and you never find out it happened." |
| **"I'm booked solid"** | "Good problem. Then it's less about *more* jobs and more about *better* ones. Showing up online lets you be pickier instead of taking whatever comes in." |
| **"I got burned by a marketing guy"** | "Yeah, what happened? … [listen, this is real information]. That's why I do it the other way — you see the thing before you pay, you own the domain, and you cancel any month you want. I'd rather earn it." |
| **"I already have a website"** | "You do — is it bringing you calls? … That's the difference. A site that sits there isn't the same as one built to make the phone ring. Two minutes, no charge, let me show you what I mean." |
| **"My nephew's building one"** | "Ha, that's how half of them start. Usually gets to about eighty percent and stalls out because he's busy with his own stuff. No shade. Mine's done in two weeks." |
| **"Send me some info"** | "I'll do better — I'll build the actual thing. What's the best email? And **Thursday or Friday** to walk through it?" |
| **"Is this AI / a scam?"** | "Ha, fair. No, it's literally me and my buddy Daniel, we're in LA. I'll text you my cell right now, you'll see we're real people." |
| **"I need to think about it"** | "Course. What's the part you'd be thinking about — the money, or whether it'll actually work?" *(then handle the real one, it's always one of those two)* |

## 9. Voicemail, then text within five minutes

**Voicemail:**
> "Hey [Name], it's Miles, 310-606-9788. I build sites for [trade] guys. Noticed your
> site's on a free Wix address and figured you'd want to know what that looks like to a
> customer. Not a sales thing — I'll just text you an example. Talk soon."

**Text:**
> "Hey [Name], Miles here — made a quick example of what your site could look like:
> [link]. Click-to-call so it rings your phone. No pressure, tell me what you think."

## 10. Call times

Trades answer **6:30–8am** and **4–6pm LOCAL**. Midday they're on a job.

The list is nationwide on purpose: start Pacific, work east, and you get **four usable
call windows in a day instead of two.** Alaska (Kenai Peninsula Fencing) is an hour
behind Pacific and extends the evening block further.

**Forty dials a week, combined. Twenty each. That's the only number that matters.**
