# Prep one call

The opening line only works if the business was looked up first. A phone room
cannot do that, which is the entire differentiator. This is the highest-frequency
job in the business: 27 dials a day.

## Input

A business name, or a rank number, from `output/main_street_callsheet.csv`.
Read that row first — it already carries trade, city, street, phone and the
`site_verified` note recorded during prospecting.

## Do this

1. **Read the call sheet row.** Do not re-derive what is already recorded there.
2. **Search the business by name.** Establish: do they have their own domain, or
   only directory listings? What do the directories say? Any obvious
   name/address/phone inconsistency between listings?
3. **Look at their site if one exists.** Specifically check the Section 4 items
   from `docs/BEFORE_AFTER.md`: is the phone a `tel:` link, is there a form,
   are hours present, does it work at phone width.
4. **Note what they are known for.** Read their reviews. The opener lands harder
   when it names the actual work.

## Output, in this order

**The opener.** One spoken sentence naming what an assistant returned, ready to
read aloud. Not a paragraph.

**What is broken.** Three bullets maximum, each a fact, each something the $1,000
fixes. Lead with anything in Section 4 — a phone number that is not tappable is
the most concrete thing you can say to an owner.

**The likely objection**, and the one-line answer. Usual ones: "I already have a
guy" (ask what they pay; $199 against a typical $499 makes its own case), "I get
all my work from referrals" (then people are searching your name and finding
directories, not you), "who else have you done this for" (nobody in your trade
yet, you would be the first, which is why it is a thousand instead of four).

**One thing to ask on the call** that cannot be looked up.

## Teach while doing it

End with a two-line **How I got there** block: which search produced the opener,
and the judgment call made. The point is that the user can run this themselves by
the tenth lead and only delegate it after that.

## Never

- Never invent what an assistant said. If it has not been run, say so and name
  the exact query to run.
- Never write more than one screen. This is read while the phone is ringing.
