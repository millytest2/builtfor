Call one: friendly, then the problem, then honest

What worked on your first real call. Warm for ten seconds, point out what you noticed, then be straight about what you do. One goal: they say yes to seeing a free website you made for them.

1. OPEN WARM (alternate A and B, note which)

A: check in

"Hey, is this [business]? ... What's going on, [name]? How we doing today?" ... "You guys staying busy?"

B: compliment and a question

"Hey [name], Miles here. Quick one: I came across [business] and saw you've got some great reviews. Do most of your new customers find you through word of mouth?"

Let them answer. Be a person for ten seconds before any business.

2. WHAT YOU NOTICED

"So I was looking around [town] and saw you guys have some great reviews [or: saw you guys are pretty new], but no website or place to book. Were you guys planning on getting that set up?"

Their site has a problem

"...I was on your site too, and [the email link at the bottom goes to a placeholder, so people who tap it never reach you]. Were you guys planning on getting that fixed?"

Only say "great reviews" if you saw them.

3. WHATEVER THEY SAY: "MAKES SENSE."

"Yeah, been meaning to.""Makes sense, it's always the thing that gets pushed."
"Don't need one, I'm busy.""Makes sense. Most crews I talk to are. It's less about more work and more about the right work, like the bigger jobs."
"Someone already made me one.""Oh nice, that's great. When's it going live? Want a free second look once it's up?"

4. BE HONEST

You made one ahead (the call sheet says Preview ready)

"Yeah, well, I'll be honest, I actually went ahead and made you one, because I wanted to help. I focus on building websites for [tree services] like yours. If you're free tomorrow or next week, I'd be happy to jump on a quick call and show you, or I can just email it over. If you like it, we can talk from there."

Not yet

"Yeah, well, I'll be honest, I focus on building websites for [tree services] like yours, and I'd be happy to put one together for you. No cost to look. I can email it over, and if you like it, we can talk from there."

5. BOOK THE LOOK

"What's the best email for you? ... And would tomorrow or early next week be better for a quick call to walk through it? Ten minutes." ... "Appreciate you, [name]."

If they ask the price

VOICEMAIL (20 SECONDS)

"Hey [name], it's Miles with Built for Main Street. I was looking around [town] and noticed you've got some great reviews but no website. I've got an idea for one, free to take a look. Give me a call at [number]. Again, [number]. Have a good one."

Same day: send the email if you have one.

Rules: Call from your business line, never your personal number. No texts to businesses. Never a range. Never promise rankings, leads or revenue. Three tries, then Dead. If you record, say so first. Tap how it went after every call.

Call two: show the preview, then one price

Before the call: have the preview link ready, and pick the plan from what you saw. One truck and a few services is Small. Most businesses are Standard. A big crew, a wide area or two locations is Bigger.

1. START EASY

"Hey [name], Miles. Thanks for making the time. Do you have the link I sent? Go ahead and pull it up, I'll walk you through it."

2. LET THEM LOOK, THEN ASK

"So this is what a customer would see when they find you. Tap call right there, it rings you. What do you think?"

Let them react. Then show why it matters:

"And here's the other half. When I asked ChatGPT who to call for tree removal in [town], it named [names]. Once this is live and your Google listing is filled out, you're the one they find. What would a few more of those calls mean for you?"

3. WHAT YOU'D DO

"To make it real, I'd finish it with your own photos and a page for each of your main jobs, get your Google listing set up properly, and set it up so Google and ChatGPT can find you. Then I keep an eye on it every month so it stays that way."

4. ONE PRICE, THEN QUIET

Small"For you it's $600 to get it live, then $99 a month to keep it running and up to date."
Standard (most)"For a business like yours it's $800 to get it live, then $199 a month. That includes a report every month on what Google and ChatGPT say about you, and asking your customers for reviews."
Bigger"For a crew your size it's $1,000 to get it live, then $300 a month. That adds same-day follow-up on every new lead and a quick call with me each month."

Then stop talking and let them think. If they want less, build fewer pages, never a lower price.

5. MAKE IT SAFE, THEN ASK

"The domain is yours. No long contract, you can cancel any time with 30 days' notice. Want me to send over the agreement and the payment link? Once that's in and you send me some photos, it's live in two weeks."

If they hesitate

"What's on your mind?"

Then listen. It's usually one worry. Answer that one and ask again.

Warm: people who already know you

Same prices, same rules. Money and photos first. Never Venmo.

CLIENT #1: FINISH, THEN ASK

"Hey [name], it's Miles. I want to finish your site properly: live, one tap to call, your Google listing done. Could you send me 10 photos by Friday?"

When it's live, show him on his phone, then:

"Would you give me two sentences on what it was like working with me?"
"Who are two other trainers or studio owners you'd trust me with?"
"To keep it hosted, updated and your listing current, it's $99 a month. Want me to set that up?"

FRIENDS: ASK FOR A NAME

"Hey [name], quick favor. I'm building websites and fixing Google listings for local businesses now. Know anyone who owns a shop, a crew, a studio, anything local, whose site is bad or who's hard to find? Just a name is huge."

They give one: "Could you text them my number and say I'll call?"

A FRIEND WHO NEEDS A SITE: NO PRESSURE

"Hey [name]! I heard you might need a website for [business]. I build them for local businesses through my business, Built for Main Street. Happy to take a look if you want. No pressure either way."

Talk about the business, not your job. If she wants it, quote the plan you picked, and make the thank-you a gift ("the first two months are on me"), never a discount.

Why warm matters: your only sale so far came through a friend. Every warm conversation ends with a testimonial or a name. They add to the cold calls. They never replace them.
