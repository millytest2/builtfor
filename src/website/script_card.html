Call one: a quick, friendly heads-up

One goal: a free ten-minute check on the calendar. Calm, curious, in no rush. Talk like you're helping a neighbor, and let them talk more than you. A "no" is fine.
Who picks up: an owner in the truck or up a tree, hoping it's a customer. Tired of marketers who promised "page one" and vanished. They relax when you're honest, specific and in no rush.

1. OPEN

"Hey, is this [name]? ... Hey [name], Miles here, with Built for Main Street. I'll be honest, it's a cold call, but it's a quick one. Got a sec, or did I catch you in the middle of a job?"

Busy: "No worries at all. Is later today better, maybe once you're off the job?" Write the time down and call then.

2. WHY YOU'RE CALLING

No website

"So I'm local, and I help crews like yours get found online. I was looking up tree removal in [town] this morning, the way a homeowner would, and asked ChatGPT who to call. It gave me [name] and [name]. You didn't come up, which surprised me with the reviews you've got. Is not having a website on purpose, or just something that never made the list?"

Their site has a problem

"So I'm local, and I help crews like yours get found online. I was on your website this morning and noticed [the email link at the bottom goes to a placeholder, so anyone who taps it never reaches you]. Figured you'd want to know. Who put the site together for you?"

Then stop and listen. Whatever they say, stay curious.

3. GET CURIOUS

"How are most of your jobs finding you right now?"

Angi or HomeAdvisor: "How's that been for you? Worth what you're paying?"
Word of mouth: "That's the best kind. And when a neighbor gives them your name, what do you think they do next?" (They look you up.)

"If you could get more of one kind of job, what would it be?"

"And when someone does look you up, do most of them just call you, or do some slip through?"

Write their answers down. They are the pitch on call two.

4. SUGGEST THE CHECK

"Here's what I'd suggest. Let me put together a quick check for you, ten minutes. I'll show you exactly what a homeowner sees when they look you up, who's showing up instead, and what I'd fix first. No cost. If it's useful, great. If not, you've lost ten minutes. Would tomorrow morning work, or is the afternoon better?"

"Perfect. What's the best email for you?" ... "Appreciate you, [name]. Talk [day]."

If they ask the price

"Totally fair. It depends on what you actually need, and I'd rather not throw out a number before I've looked properly. After the check you'll get one straight price, and it won't change."

When they push back

VOICEMAIL (20 SECONDS)

"Hey [name], it's Miles with Built for Main Street. I was looking up tree services in [town] this morning and noticed something about how [business] shows up online. Worth a two-minute chat. Give me a call at [number]. Again, [number]. Have a good one."

Same day: send the email if you have one.

Rules: Call from your business line, never your personal number. No texts to businesses. Never a range. Never promise rankings, leads or revenue. Three tries, then Dead. If you record, say so first. Tap how it went after every call.

Call two: walk them through it, then one price

Before the call: pick the plan from what you saw. One truck and a few services is Small. Most businesses are Standard. A big crew, a wide area or two locations is Bigger.

1. START EASY

"Hey [name], Miles. Thanks for making the time. So I looked you up the way a homeowner in [town] would. Mind if I walk you through what came up?"

2. SHOW, THEN ASK

"When I asked ChatGPT who to call for tree removal in [town], it said [their answer]. On Google, the first three were [names]. And on your listing, [what's missing]."

"What do you make of that?"

Let them say it. When they name the problem themselves, you don't have to sell it.

3. WHAT YOU'D DO

"Here's what I'd do for you. A real website with a page for each of your main jobs and the areas you cover. Your Google listing filled out properly. One tap to call you. And a simple way to ask your happy customers for reviews. Then I keep an eye on it every month so it stays that way."

4. ONE PRICE, THEN QUIET

Small"For you it's $600 to set it all up, then $99 a month to keep it running and up to date."
Standard (most)"For a business like yours it's $800 to set it all up, then $199 a month. That includes a report every month on what Google and ChatGPT say about you, and the review requests."
Bigger"For a crew your size it's $1,000 to set it up, then $300 a month. That adds same-day follow-up on every new lead and a quick call with me each month."

Then stop talking and let them think. If they want less, build fewer pages, never a lower price.

5. MAKE IT SAFE, THEN ASK

"The domain is yours. No long contract, you can cancel any time with 30 days' notice. Want me to send over the agreement and the payment link so we can get started?"

If they hesitate

"What's on your mind?"

Then listen. It's usually one worry. Answer that one and ask again.

Warm: people who already know you

Same prices, same rules. Money and photos first. Never Venmo.

CLIENT #1: FINISH, THEN ASK

"Hey [name], it's Miles. I want to finish your site properly: live, one tap to call, your Google listing done. Could you send me 10 photos by Friday?"

When it's live, show him on his phone, then:

"Would you give me two sentences on what it was like working with me?"
"Who are two other trainers or studio owners you'd trust me with?"
"To keep it hosted, updated and your listing current, it's $99 a month. Want me to set that up?"

FRIENDS: ASK FOR A NAME

"Hey [name], quick favor. I'm building websites and fixing Google listings for local businesses now. Know anyone who owns a shop, a crew, a studio, anything local, whose site is bad or who's hard to find? Just a name is huge."

They give one: "Could you text them my number and say I'll call?"

A FRIEND WHO NEEDS A SITE: NO PRESSURE

"Hey [name]! I heard you might need a website for [business]. I build them for local businesses through my business, Built for Main Street. Happy to take a look if you want. No pressure either way."

Talk about the business, not your job. If she wants it, quote the plan you picked, and make the thank-you a gift ("the first two months are on me"), never a discount.

Why warm matters: your only sale so far came through a friend. Every warm conversation ends with a testimonial or a name. They add to the cold calls. They never replace them.
