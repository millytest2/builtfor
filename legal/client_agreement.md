# Service Agreement — Built for Main Street

> **This is a plain-English starting draft built on standard service-agreement terms.
> It is not legal advice.** Have a lawyer read it once — an hour, about $300–500 — and
> then use it a hundred times. The clauses most worth their attention are §6 (IP and
> ownership), §8 (liability) and §9 (termination).

---

**Between:** Built for Main Street ("we", "us") and **[CLIENT BUSINESS NAME]** ("you")
**Date:** ______________

## 1. What we're building

A website for your business, plus setup of your Google Business Profile, a call
tracking number, click-to-call, and a contact form.

**Deliverables:** ☐ 1-page site ☐ 3–5 page site
**Live within 14 days** of the date we receive your photos, service list, and logins.
Not from the date you pay — from the date we have what we need to start.

## 2. What you pay

| | |
|---|---|
| Setup, one time | **$400** |
| Care plan, monthly | **$349/mo**, first month included |
| Alternative: site only, no care plan | **$600** one time, no ongoing support |

Setup is due when you sign. The care plan starts the day your site goes live and bills
monthly to the card on file. **First month is included**, so your first charge is 30
days after launch.

## 3. What the care plan covers

- Hosting, SSL certificate, daily backups, security
- Uptime monitoring, and we fix it if it breaks
- Your call tracking number, and a monthly count of the calls your site produced
- **One small change per month** — hours, a photo, a service, a phone number
- Keeping your site's info matched to your Google profile

## 4. What it does not cover

Redesigns, new pages, new sections, e-commerce, booking systems, blog writing, social
media posting, paid advertising, or ongoing SEO campaigns. We'll quote those
separately and you're free to say no.

## 5. What we need from you

Photos, your service list, hours, service area, and access to your Google Business
Profile. **If we don't have these, we can't start the clock.** We'll chase you twice
and then pause the project until you're ready. Nothing expires.

## 6. Who owns what

**You do.** On full payment of the setup fee:

- **The domain is registered in your name.** You own it outright, today, not on exit.
- The website content, copy, images and design are yours.
- If you leave, we send you a full export and transfer the domain. No fee, no hostage
  situation, no thirty-day waiting period.

We keep the right to show your site in our portfolio and to mention we built it.
Say the word and we won't.

We're a **Manager** on your Google Business Profile, never the Owner. That account
stays yours and you can remove us any time.

**You confirm you have the right to use every photo, logo and review you give us.**
If a photo isn't yours, tell us and we'll source one properly.

## 7. The call tracking number

The tracking number goes on your website and forwards to your phone. It shows us how
many calls your site actually produced. Your real number stays the primary on your
Google listing. **If you cancel, we'll port the tracking number to you on request** —
just ask before the account closes.

## 8. Limits

We build the site and keep it running. **We can't promise a specific number of calls,
customers or dollars** — nobody honestly can, and anyone who does is selling you
something.

If something goes wrong, our total liability is capped at what you paid us in the
previous three months. We're not liable for lost profits or indirect losses.

The site is provided as-is once launched. We'll fix faults; we can't guarantee
uninterrupted uptime, because nobody controls the whole internet.

## 9. Ending it

**Cancel any time. No contract, no notice period, no cancellation fee.**

Email or text us and we'll stop the billing at the end of the current month. You keep
the site, you keep the domain, we send you the files. If you're mid-project and cancel
before launch, the setup fee covers the work done and isn't refundable.

We can end it too, with 30 days' notice, and we'll hand everything over the same way.

## 10. The boring bits

Governed by the laws of the State of California. If we disagree about something, we
talk first, and mediate before anyone sues. This is the whole agreement; changes go in
writing. Neither of us is the other's employee or partner.
