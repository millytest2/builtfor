# Contractor Agreement — Built for Main Street

> **Plain-English starting draft, not legal advice.** Get it reviewed once with the
> client agreement. **Do not pay a contractor a dollar before this is signed and you
> have their W-9.** §3 is the reason this document exists.

---

**Between:** Built for Main Street ("Company") and **[CONTRACTOR NAME]** ("Contractor")
**Date:** ______________

## 1. The work

Contractor builds websites from the Company's templates and build briefs, per the
delivery checklist in `docs/delivery_runsheet.md`. Each job is assigned individually.
Contractor may accept or decline any job.

## 2. Pay

**$[___] per completed site**, or **$[___]/hour**, invoiced monthly.
Paid within 15 days of invoice.

A site is complete when it passes the Company's AFTER audit and the client has
approved the preview. Revisions inside the one agreed round are part of the job.

## 3. Ownership — the important one

**All work product is work made for hire and belongs to the Company.** To the extent
anything isn't automatically work for hire, Contractor irrevocably assigns to the
Company all right, title and interest in it, including all intellectual property.

Contractor confirms every element delivered is either original, properly licensed, or
supplied by the client. **No stock images without a license. No scraped photos. No
copied copy.** Contractor indemnifies the Company for any claim arising from material
they introduced.

*Without this clause, your contractor owns the site you just sold to a client, and you
find out about it at the worst possible moment.*

## 4. Independent contractor

Contractor is not an employee. They control their own hours, methods and equipment,
may work for others, and are responsible for their own taxes and insurance. No
benefits, no withholding. **The Company issues a 1099-NEC for any year paying $600 or
more**, and Contractor provides a completed **W-9 before the first payment.**

## 5. Confidentiality and clients

Contractor keeps client information, logins, pricing and Company methods confidential,
during and after the engagement.

**Contractor will not solicit or accept direct work from any Company client** they were
introduced to through this engagement, for 12 months after the last job. Nothing here
prevents Contractor from working in this industry generally — this is about Company
clients specifically.

## 6. Security

Client logins live in the Company's password manager and nowhere else. Not in email,
not in text messages, not in a personal notes app. Contractor's access is revoked at
the end of the engagement and they confirm deletion of local copies.

## 7. Ending it

Either side can end this on 14 days' written notice. Jobs already assigned get
finished and paid. §3, §5 and §6 survive.

## 8. The boring bits

California law. The whole agreement. Changes in writing.
